# Dirtyworks.ai — private conversation brief

**Status:** Draft for interviews; not a public promise or final website copy

## When the answer lives with one person

Growing service companies often have useful procedures, project history, specifications, templates, customer rules, and operating knowledge. The problem is getting the current answer when someone needs it.

Staff search several folders and systems, ask the same experienced people, follow an old version, or recreate work that already exists. A new AI licence does not fix source ownership, permissions, contradictions, adoption, or ongoing quality on its own.

Dirtyworks.ai is testing a managed knowledge operations service for established Alberta professional-services and energy-service SMBs.

## What it does

We start with one important group of company questions. We map the real work, approved sources, owners, permissions, risk, and a baseline. If the use is feasible, we configure a customer-owned business AI/knowledge platform so approved staff can ask supported questions and see the sources behind the answer.

Then we operate it: quality checks, source freshness, access, support, training, reporting, vendor changes, and a managed improvement backlog.

Repeated questions can reveal work worth repairing, structuring, assisting, or automating. That decision comes later and remains separately governed.

## Good first fit

- 20–150 employees with shared operating knowledge.
- A repeated question domain affects onboarding, estimating, project/service delivery, customer response, billing, or continuity.
- Useful approved source material already exists.
- An executive sponsor and source owner will participate.
- The first role group and source scope can be bounded.

For professional-services firms, the first scope focuses on internal methods and administration—not client records, employment decisions, or professional conclusions. For energy-service firms, it focuses on commercial/project knowledge—not safety-critical, engineering, operational-control, or regulatory decisions.

## Not the first fit

- A public or unsupervised chatbot.
- High-impact legal, employment, safety, health, or financial decisions.
- A promise that undocumented knowledge will appear automatically.
- Company-wide content cleanup or unlimited automation disguised as support.
- Guaranteed perfect answers.

## Starting engagement

The proposed **Knowledge Reliability Review** is a paid 7–10 business-day assessment of one answer domain and up to three candidate source systems. It produces a source/permission/risk map, real evaluation questions, baseline, launch recommendation, scope, and price. The current test price is approximately **$2,500 CAD**.

A review may recommend launching, repairing sources first, capturing undocumented knowledge, using a simpler non-AI solution, or stopping.

## Research conversation

Before selling the service, Dirtyworks.ai is speaking with owners, managing partners, and operations leaders about the last time staff could not find or verify a current operating answer. The useful starting point is a recent event—not a generic AI discussion.
