# Go-to-market strategy

**Version:** 0.2  
**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**Status:** Ready for direct-cohort and MSP-partner validation

## Objective

Win the first five managed customers while learning whether the problem, offer, pricing, and delivery model repeat. The first go-to-market system optimizes for evidence and references, not maximum lead volume.

## Beachhead

Use two market lanes without marketing horizontally:

1. **Direct lane:** one concentrated 10-interview cohort selected from accounting firms, HR/advisory firms, or Calgary-area oil-and-gas service/vendor companies, followed by a second 10-interview cohort chosen from the evidence.
2. **MSP lane:** a separate partner-discovery process and one named-customer design pilot through the available MSP relationship.

The direct cohorts establish buyer/problem evidence. The MSP lane establishes channel demand, responsibilities, wholesale economics, and customer-access requirements. Do not count a partner’s enthusiasm as customer validation.

Do not market horizontally to “all SMBs” until one problem pattern, message, and case study repeat.

## Positioning

### Primary message

> Your company knows more than it can find. Dirtyworks.ai gives your team reliable answers from approved company information—and keeps the sources, permissions, quality, and rollout managed.

### Supporting proof themes

- Find and verify current procedures, project history, specifications, templates, and commercial rules.
- Reduce waiting for the one person who knows.
- Give new staff a faster path to approved answers.
- See what is missing, contradictory, or repeatedly requested.
- Keep company AI governed without building an internal department.

### Language to avoid

- “Transform your business with AI.”
- “Unlock the power of your data.”
- “Eliminate hallucinations.”
- “Automate everything.”
- “Single source of truth” unless the source model actually supports that claim.
- Claims of guaranteed ROI, perfect accuracy, or complete security.

## Trigger events

Prioritize accounts where urgency is visible:

- senior employee retirement, departure, or extended absence;
- rapid hiring or a new branch/crew;
- acquisition or merger;
- new practice-management, ERP, project, field-service, or document platform;
- quality or compliance issue caused by an old procedure;
- repeated customer response delays;
- owner or manager publicly discussing AI policy or productivity;
- change in leadership with a mandate to standardize operations.

## Founder-led demand system

### Motion 1 — Existing network

Ask trusted operators, advisers, MSPs, fractional executives, accountants, and software implementers for problem conversations, not generic referrals:

> I’m researching where established service companies lose time because the current answer is hard to find or lives with one experienced person. Who has dealt with that recently?

This wording produces a relevant introduction without forcing the contact to understand the category.

### Motion 2 — Named-account outreach

Build the first list around 50 companies in the selected direct subsegment. A half-time founder can research and work that cohort with sufficient depth; expand toward 100 only when the message shows signal. For each account, record size, systems, trigger event, likely buyer, warm path, and a specific operating hypothesis.

Example:

Professional-services example:

> As firms grow, practice and operations leaders often become the answer desk for approved methods, templates, software procedures, and engagement administration. I’m researching the last-mile problem of finding and verifying those answers—not automating professional judgment or client decisions. Would a 25-minute working conversation be useful?

Energy-services example:

> As project and service companies grow, commercial and project teams often search across old jobs, client requirements, templates, and internal systems before they can act. I’m researching that knowledge-access problem separately from safety-critical or engineering decisions. Would a 25-minute working conversation be useful?

### Motion 3 — Operator roundtables

After 10 interviews, run a small session around “What stops when the person who knows is away?” Use anonymized patterns and a self-assessment. The call to action is the paid Knowledge Reliability Review, not a broad AI workshop.

### Motion 4 — MSP partner design pilot

Use the live MSP opportunity to test referral, co-managed/co-branded, and white-label structures. Prefer the lightest structure that gives Dirtyworks.ai enough direct delivery access and evidence. The partner message is:

> You retain the infrastructure, security, and account relationship you choose to own. Dirtyworks.ai supplies the managed knowledge method, launch, quality, and ongoing operations. We define sales, support, customer access, economics, data roles, and escalation before taking a customer live.

The full commercial and responsibility design is in the [MSP channel strategy](../../02-strategy-and-research/channels/msp-channel-strategy.md).

## Interview system

Use recent events, not opinions:

- Tell me about the last time someone could not find the current answer.
- Where did they look, who did they interrupt, and what happened while they waited?
- Which role absorbs these questions?
- What questions repeat during a new employee’s first 90 days?
- When did two people last use different versions of the same instruction or template?
- What cannot proceed when one specific person is away?
- What systems and folders were involved?
- What is the consequence: elapsed time, rework, delayed revenue, risk, or frustration?
- What have you already tried?
- Who owns fixing it, and what would make it a priority now?

Do not show the proposed solution until the event, frequency, impact, current alternative, buyer, and urgency are understood.

## Qualification

Score each opportunity from 0–2 on eight dimensions:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repeated event | Vague | Occasional | Recent and recurring |
| Economic effect | None | Plausible | Measurable/material |
| Existing knowledge | Mostly oral | Mixed | Useful approved sources exist |
| Scope | Company-wide/unclear | Some boundary | One clear domain and role group |
| Executive sponsor | None | Interested | Accountable and active |
| Knowledge owner | None | Candidate | Named and available |
| Access/risk | Prohibited/high | Needs remediation | Bounded and acceptable |
| Timing/budget | No priority | Future | Paid review now |

Minimum score for a proposal: 12/16 with no zero in sponsor, access/risk, or knowledge owner. A score is a decision aid, not a substitute for judgment.

## Sales stages and exit criteria

| Stage | Required exit evidence |
|---|---|
| Target | ICP fit, likely buyer, and a reason for contact |
| Problem conversation | Recent event, affected role, consequence, current workaround, and next stakeholder |
| Qualified | Score threshold, named sponsor/owner, bounded domain, timing, and willingness to consider paid review |
| Review proposed | Fixed scope, access requirements, schedule, price, and decision process sent |
| Review sold | Signed order, payment, people and access scheduled |
| Launch proposed | Review findings, launch scope, risks, acceptance tests, recurring price, and customer obligations agreed |
| Launch sold | Signed order and milestone payment |
| Managed customer | Production acceptance and recurring service active |
| Expansion | Measured gap classified and separate business case approved |

## Initial funnel hypotheses

Over the first 12 weeks:

| Activity/outcome | Target |
|---|---:|
| Named accounts researched | 50 in cohort 1; expand based on signal |
| Warm introduction requests | 30 |
| Relevant buyer/problem conversations | 20 |
| Qualified opportunities | 10 |
| Paid reviews | 3–5 |
| Launches | 2–3 |
| Recurring conversions | 2+ |
| MSP partner discovery sessions | 1–2 |
| Named MSP pilot customers | 1 |

These numbers are test assumptions, not forecasts. Track conversion and the reason for every loss. A low response rate may be a targeting or message problem; a low paid-review rate after strong conversations is more likely an offer, urgency, trust, or price problem.

## Content and website architecture

The first website should be a credibility and qualification tool, not a large content property.

1. **Home:** “Your company knows more than it can find,” the operating promise, how the managed service works, boundaries, and call to action.
2. **Company Knowledge:** review, launch, ongoing operations, scope, and fit.
3. **How it works:** approved sources → reliable answers → measured gaps → managed improvements.
4. **Who it is for:** professional services, energy services, fit and non-fit signals.
5. **Trust:** customer ownership, access controls, citations, human accountability, privacy, portability, and limitations.
6. **Insights:** a small number of event-based operator articles.
7. **Book a problem review:** screening questions before scheduling.
8. **For MSPs:** referral, co-managed, and white-label capability without premature public rate promises.

The sponsor has approved Dirtyworks.ai as the public brand. Build a private site preview from the approved message architecture after selecting cohort 1; publish only after at least five buyer interviews and the MSP discovery confirm that the claims and channel language do not create material confusion. The [brand and message strategy](../../04-marketing-and-brand/messaging/brand-and-message-strategy.md) defines the working tagline and trust tests.

## Proof system

Case studies should show:

- the role group and bounded question domain;
- source and permission scope without exposing confidential details;
- baseline and measurement method;
- answer reliability by question class;
- repeated use and qualitative behavior change;
- one operating outcome such as time-to-answer or expert interruptions;
- unresolved gaps and what was deliberately not automated;
- customer quote approved for the exact claim.

Never present prompts, total questions, or model usage as ROI by themselves.

## Weekly GTM operating cadence

- Monday: account selection, trigger research, and outreach preparation.
- Tuesday–Thursday: problem interviews, follow-up, partner conversations, and proposals.
- Friday: pipeline review, loss reasons, message evidence, content capture, and next experiment.
- Monthly: cohort conversion, cycle time, acquisition effort, willingness-to-pay, and segment review.

Every conversation updates the evidence base. Do not continually rewrite positioning from isolated feedback; change it when a pattern repeats.
