# MSP partner discovery kit

**Purpose:** determine whether the live MSP opportunity should be referral, co-managed/co-branded, or full white-label, and whether a real customer pilot exists.  
**Meeting length:** 60 minutes  
**Outcome:** a qualified next step, not a generic partnership agreement

## Pre-meeting request

Ask the MSP to bring:

- owner/practice lead and the person who would sell the offer;
- approximate managed customer count and target industries/sizes;
- examples of current customer AI requests;
- current Microsoft/Google/cloud/security capabilities;
- one or two possible pilot accounts, anonymized if necessary;
- expectations for brand, contract, margin, support, and customer access.

Do not request customer documents, credentials, or personal information during partner discovery.

## Agenda

| Time | Topic | Intended evidence |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Why now | Actual customer demand or strategic hypothesis |
| 5–15 min | Customer base and recent requests | ICP overlap and recognizable events |
| 15–25 min | Current capability and gap | What the MSP can own versus needs supplied |
| 25–38 min | Partner-model choices | Reason for referral, co-managed, or white-label preference |
| 38–48 min | Named pilot and service boundary | Real customer path and acceptable first domain |
| 48–56 min | Economics, contracts, support, risk | Commercial feasibility |
| 56–60 min | Decision and next action | Owner, artifact, and date |

## Discovery questions

### Demand and customer fit

- What are clients actually asking for when they say AI?
- Which request happened most recently, and what did the client want accomplished?
- Which customer roles and industries ask most often?
- How many managed clients fit 20–150 employees?
- Which clients have fragmented knowledge, repeated questions, onboarding pressure, or key-person dependence?
- What have you already sold or delivered?

### Partner motivation

- Why partner instead of building the capability or referring it out?
- Why is white-label important? What would be lost with “powered by Dirtyworks.ai” or a co-managed specialist?
- Is the priority revenue, retention, competitive defence, customer demand, or internal capability?
- What result would make the partnership worthwhile in six months?

### Sales and commercial ownership

- Who finds, qualifies, and closes the customer?
- Who creates and approves claims, proposals, and scope?
- Who signs the customer contract, invoices, carries bad debt, and handles renewals?
- What gross margin does the MSP expect, and what work will it perform for that margin?
- Is there a named pipeline or only general portfolio potential?
- Is exclusivity expected? What minimum revenue would support it?

### Delivery and customer access

- Can Dirtyworks.ai meet the buyer, users, source owners, and access owner under the agreed brand?
- Who performs the Knowledge Reliability Review?
- Who owns tenant identity, groups, security, licences, and connector permissions?
- Who owns source truth, evaluation, release approval, training, and value reporting?
- Can anonymized operational evidence be retained to improve the generalized method?
- Can the customer approve an attributed or anonymous case study separately?

### Support and incidents

- What does the MSP’s service desk handle today?
- Can it own tier-1 intake, identity/access basics, customer status, and ticket records?
- How will quality, source, permission, connector, workflow, privacy, and security incidents route?
- What response promises does the MSP already make?
- Who may suspend the service or workflow?
- How are after-hours issues handled and paid for?

### Security, privacy, and contracts

- What customer contract, security standard, insurance, and vendor-review obligations already exist?
- Does the MSP disclose subcontractors and subprocessors to customers?
- Which party is processor/service provider under the customer’s instructions?
- What data residency, retention, logging, deletion, and breach requirements recur?
- What confidentiality, non-solicitation, IP, liability, and transition terms are expected?

### Named pilot

- Which one customer has a recent problem and accountable sponsor?
- What non-authoritative question domain could be reviewed first?
- Which 1–3 sources are likely involved?
- What personal, professional, safety, or regulated information can be excluded?
- Will the customer pay for the reliability review?
- Who will make the introduction and by what date?

## Decision record

Complete within one business day:

- partner and participants;
- evidence of customer demand;
- selected/provisional model and why;
- named pilot account and first domain;
- responsibilities requiring clarification;
- proposed economics and assumptions;
- legal/privacy/security issues;
- exclusivity or customer-ownership request;
- next artifact, owner, and date;
- explicit stop/nurture reason if no pilot proceeds.

## Valid next outcomes

1. **Referral pilot:** MSP introduces; Dirtyworks.ai sells and contracts.
2. **Co-managed design pilot:** parties jointly sell; responsibility schedule precedes proposal.
3. **White-label design pilot:** MSP is prime; paid enablement/commitment and customer-access path are required.
4. **Nurture:** fit exists but no named customer or timing.
5. **Decline:** economics, access, risk, or strategic conflict is unacceptable.
