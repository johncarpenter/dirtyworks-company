# Dirtyworks.ai website and presentation blueprint

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**Purpose:** Give a design engine enough content hierarchy, narrative, component behavior, and example copy to produce the first website and presentation system  
**Creative territory:** [PROOF / WORK](../brand/creative-direction-and-design-brief.md)

> **Website expansion note — 2026-08-25:** The [marketing website content and layout handoff](marketing-website-content-and-layout.md) supersedes the website sections below. It reframes the public offer around the full AI MSP / managed AI operations service, with the product catalogue as one layer and knowledge reliability as a differentiator. The presentation/deck system in this document remains active.

## Shared narrative

The website and deck should tell the same argument at different speeds:

```text
The answer exists, but work still waits
                 ↓
AI access is not the same as an operated capability
                 ↓
Reliable answers require sources, permissions, owners and testing
                 ↓
Dirtyworks.ai sets up and operates that layer
                 ↓
Start with one bounded question domain
                 ↓
Measure value; repair gaps; automate only proven work
```

The website lets a buyer choose depth. The deck controls sequence. Neither should begin with company history, platform logos, or a feature list.

## Website objectives

1. Make a qualified buyer recognize the problem in ten seconds.
2. Explain the managed difference without technical category language.
3. Show enough method and restraint to earn a conversation about company information.
4. Separate direct customers from MSP partners without presenting two different companies.
5. Convert to a diagnostic conversation or Knowledge Reliability Review.

## Website information architecture

### Primary navigation

- `WORK` — the problem and operating outcomes;
- `METHOD` — review, launch, operate, improve;
- `TRUST` — ownership, sources, permissions, human accountability, incidents, portability;
- `FOR MSPs` — referral, co-managed, and white-label partnership;

### Utility/footer

- About / founder credibility;
- fit and non-fit;
- privacy;
- terms;
- accessibility;
- contact;
- Alberta / Canada operating location;
- LinkedIn or selected professional channel;
- current document/version marker as a visual device, not a legal claim.

## Homepage blueprint

### 01 — Hero: interruption

**Display:**

> YOUR COMPANY  
> KNOWS MORE THAN  
> IT CAN FIND.

**Descriptor:**

> Managed knowledge and AI operations for Alberta businesses.

**Support:**

> Dirtyworks.ai gives your team reliable answers from approved company information—and operates the sources, permissions, quality, support, and improvements behind them.

**Primary CTA:** `SHOW US WHERE WORK GETS STUCK`  
**Secondary CTA:** `SEE THE WORK BEHIND THE ANSWER`

**Visual behavior:** Oversized headline with a source strip emerging from behind `FIND`. The descriptor is a small mono label. No product screenshot.

### 02 — Problem: the invisible answer desk

**Heading:**

> “ASK THE PERSON WHO KNOWS” IS NOT A KNOWLEDGE SYSTEM.

**Evidence fragments:**

- The current procedure is somewhere in SharePoint.
- The last project solved this. Nobody can find it.
- New staff keep asking the same five people.
- Two teams are using different versions.
- A public AI tool is already in the workflow. Nobody owns it.

**Copy:**

> The cost does not arrive as one invoice. It appears as searching, interruptions, waiting, rework, inconsistent answers, slow onboarding, and dependence on whoever remembers.

**Visual behavior:** Five fragments behave like misaligned source sheets. On scroll they align into a compact evidence register.

### 03 — Contrarian frame

**Display:**

> AI WAS THE EASY PART.

**Support:**

> A licence can expose an interface. It cannot decide which source is approved, fix contradictory instructions, assign an owner, test who can see what, train the team, or handle the Monday-morning failure.

**Graphic:** Redact `THE AI` from a list and reveal `THE OPERATION`.

### 04 — Method

**Heading:**

> GOOD ANSWERS BEGIN BEFORE THE QUESTION.

**Sequence:**

1. `REVIEW` — one question domain, real events, sources, owners, permissions, risk, baseline.
2. `LAUNCH` — configure the customer-owned platform, evaluate real questions, test access, train a bounded group.
3. `OPERATE` — monitor quality, freshness, support, use, cost, incidents, and change.
4. `IMPROVE` — repair knowledge, structure data, assist work, or automate only when evidence supports it.

**Visual behavior:** Not four equal cards. Use a vertical work order with annotations and a loop returning improvement to review.

### 05 — What managed means

**Heading:**

> SOMEBODY OWNS MONDAY MORNING.

**Two-field comparison:**

| A tool | An operated capability |
|---|---|
| Connected | Sources approved and owned |
| Answers | Answer classes evaluated |
| User access | Permissions tested |
| Training link | Role-specific rollout and support |
| Usage count | Quality, gaps, risk, and value reviewed |
| Vendor update | Change assessed and regression tested |
| Cancel button | Portable offboarding package |

### 06 — Trust

**Heading:**

> “I DON’T KNOW” IS A FEATURE.

**Trust statements:**

- Customer-owned technology and data by default.
- Approved sources with visible evidence.
- Least privilege and permission tests.
- Human accountability for consequential decisions.
- Explicit exclusions, incidents, changes, and offboarding.
- No claim of perfect answers or complete security.

**CTA:** `READ THE TRUST MODEL`

### 07 — Fit

**Heading:**

> START WHERE THE ANSWER MATTERS.

**Professional services:** approved firm methods, templates, software procedures, engagement administration, onboarding. Exclude client records and professional/employment decisions initially.

**Energy services:** commercial and project administration, client requirements, internal systems, project closeout. Exclude safety, engineering, field-control, and regulatory decisions initially.

**Visual behavior:** Two large editorial spreads, not industry-icon cards.

### 08 — Offer

**Heading:**

> ONE DOMAIN. ONE DECISION. THEN BUILD.

**Offer:**

> The Knowledge Reliability Review is a paid 7–10 business-day assessment of one question domain and up to three candidate sources. It maps value, ownership, permissions, source quality, risk, real evaluation questions, and a recommended next move.

**Possible conclusions:** `LAUNCH` · `REPAIR FIRST` · `CAPTURE KNOWLEDGE` · `USE A SIMPLER TOOL` · `STOP`

**Price treatment:** Display the current $2,500 hypothesis only when the commercial decision is ready for public testing. Otherwise use `PAID FIXED-SCOPE REVIEW`.

### 09 — MSP partner lane

**Heading:**

> KEEP THE ACCOUNT. ADD THE PRACTICE.

**Copy:**

> Dirtyworks.ai gives MSPs a managed knowledge and AI operations capability without requiring them to build the delivery method from scratch. Referral, co-managed, and white-label structures are available when sales, customer access, support, data roles, margin, and liability are explicit.

**CTA:** `DESIGN A PARTNER PILOT`

### 10 — Point of view / manifesto

**Display:**

> NO THEATRE.  
> NO MYSTERY.  
> WORK THAT WORKS.

Use the short brand story from the [brand platform and voice](../brand/brand-platform-and-voice.md).

### 11 — Conversion

**Heading:**

> SHOW US THE LAST ANSWER YOUR TEAM HAD TO CHASE.

**Form fields:**

- name, company, role, email;
- what was the person trying to do?;
- where did they look?;
- who did they finally ask?;
- which systems were involved?;
- optional: company size and MSP relationship.

Do not ask for customer documents or sensitive data in the website form.

## Secondary page briefs

### Method page

Expand review → launch → operate → improve. Include acceptance, failure paths, customer responsibilities, and a sample monthly operating record. Visual anchor: annotated lifecycle work order.

### Trust page

Lead with limitations before credentials. Cover ownership, sources, permissions, evaluation, personal/sensitive information, human review, telemetry, incidents, vendors, portability, and prohibited early uses. Visual anchor: a public-facing control register.

### For MSPs page

Show three partner models, the responsibility seam, qualification, pilot sequence, and why licences alone are not the practice. Do not publish wholesale percentages before live-partner validation. Visual anchor: split work order with named accountable parties.

### Notes page

Editorial index rather than blog-card grid. Each note shows thesis, evidence type, date, and reading time.

Initial article titles:

- RAG is becoming a feature. Knowledge ownership is still work.
- “Ask Sarah” is your most expensive undocumented system.
- Permission is part of the answer.
- Before you automate the repeated question, find out why it repeats.
- A managed AI service needs a failure path.
- What an MSP owns—and what a knowledge operator should.

## Website component inventory

Build only components with a distinct content job:

- oversized declaration;
- evidence rail/source fragment;
- annotated comparison;
- work-order lifecycle;
- trust/control register;
- owner/status row;
- pull quote in editorial serif;
- hard CTA band;
- proof label/status stamp;
- article index row;
- bounded fit/non-fit field;
- case-study metric with method/source;
- diagnostic form.

Avoid generic feature card, testimonial carousel, logo cloud, gradient stat card, floating chat widget, and animated particle background unless later evidence gives them a concrete job.

## Website content requirements before public launch

- approved company/legal name and contact details;
- privacy and website terms reviewed;
- service claims aligned to actual production readiness;
- founder credibility and relevant experience copy;
- one real or clearly labelled illustrative example;
- form data-handling path;
- accessibility and performance review;
- no fabricated customer logos, metrics, interface screenshots, or testimonials;
- MSP claims approved for the actual partner structure.

## Presentation system

### Core deck purpose

Create a 12–15 slide founder-led business/customer narrative that can be adapted for:

1. qualified SMB buyers;
2. MSP partner discussions;
3. investors or operators evaluating the business-in-a-box.

Do not force all audiences into the same final slides. Slides 1–8 establish the common argument; the ending branches.

### 01 — Cover / interruption

> YOUR COMPANY KNOWS MORE THAN IT CAN FIND.

Small label: `DIRTYWORKS.AI / MANAGED KNOWLEDGE + AI OPERATIONS`

### 09 — Market wedge

Professional services + energy services + MSP distribution, with hypotheses clearly separated from validated evidence.

### 10 — Business model

Paid review, launch, managed recurring service, expansion, direct/channel economics.

### 11 — Defensibility

Trust, operating relationship, evaluation patterns, source practices, vertical methods, workflow evidence, and partner distribution—not the chat interface.

Commodity product, poor sources, permission leakage, wrong answers, weak adoption, scope explosion, channel dependence.

### 15 — Decision / ask

State the exact capital, partner, customer, or operating decision required. Never end with only `QUESTIONS?`.

## Slide archetypes

The design engine should produce:

1. **Declaration:** 2–10 words, extreme scale, one proof label.
2. **Evidence:** one metric or fact, visible source/method, small interpretation.
3. **Event path:** sequence of actions with elapsed-time or interruption marks.
4. **Mechanism:** system diagram with source, permission, owner, AI, answer, human, gap, and loop.
5. **Comparison:** two fields with one decisive contrast.
6. **Work order:** scope, owner, status, acceptance, next action.
7. **Case:** before/event/intervention/result/limitation.
8. **Quote:** Instrument Serif statement with attributed context.
9. **Decision:** options, tradeoffs, recommendation, requested choice.
10. **Appendix/data:** dense but readable record with folio and source rail.

## Deck composition rules

- No repeated top-left title plus six bullets.
- No more than one primary claim per slide.
- Use no more than 30–45 words on declaration slides.
- Evidence slides may be dense but require a visible reading path.
- Alternate sparse and dense slides to create pace.
- Every statistic has source/date/method.
- Every illustrative example says `ILLUSTRATIVE`.
- Avoid tiny screenshots; redraw the mechanism or crop one decisive detail.
- Use notes for nuance the slide cannot carry.
- PowerPoint/Google Slides versions must remain editable and use embedded/licensed fonts or defined fallbacks.

## Handoff acceptance criteria

The design package is ready when:

- a stranger can identify the offer from the homepage hero in ten seconds;
- the visual direction cannot be mistaken for a generic AI SaaS brand;
- trust and incident content looks native to the identity, not appended legal material;
- professional-services, energy-services, and MSP examples all fit without changing brands;
- website mobile layouts retain the deliberate irregularity without collision;
- deck slides remain editable and legible on a conference-room screen;
- colour, type, motion, focus, and texture pass accessibility review;
- the system includes enough templates to move quickly but enough range to avoid template monotony;
- claims, sources, illustrative examples, and unvalidated hypotheses are visually distinguishable.
