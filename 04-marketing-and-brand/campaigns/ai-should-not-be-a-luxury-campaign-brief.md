**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-26  
**Status:** Social campaign experiment; not an approved replacement for the core photography system  
**Campaign type:** Luxury-fashion satire / product and model films  
**Primary formats:** 6–15 second vertical product films, fashion campaign stills, product-detail loops  
**Product reference:** [Luxury AI product template](../assets/photography/campaign-concepts/luxury-ai-product-template-v1.png)

## Purpose

Test a distinctive visual campaign that treats AI products like extravagant luxury goods while preserving a pro-AI position:

> The tools can be useful and desirable. The unmanaged licences, overlap, consumption, support gaps, and renewal decisions can still become extravagant.

The campaign does not argue that AI vendors or their products are inherently overpriced. It dramatizes the cost of buying capability without an operating model.

This is initially a social and campaign-landing-page system. Do not replace the website's core photography direction unless repeated tests show that the visual territory attracts the intended buyers, communicates cost control accurately, and remains usable beyond a single joke.

## The premise

Every AI product is photographed and filmed with the impossible seriousness of a fragrance, couture, jewellery, or automotive campaign:

- obsidian glass;
- veined marble;
- theatrical mist;
- remote locations;
- immaculate models;
- severe black-and-white lighting;
- one precise Dirtyworks.ai orange cable;
- an invoice ribbon that refuses to stop;
- one modest, useful output.

The luxury is desirable at first glance. The excess reveals itself a beat later.

The recurring contrast is:

```text
Beautiful capability
        versus
Extravagant operation
```

## Strategic message

### Audience takeaway

> Keep the useful AI. Remove the unmanaged excess around it.

### Connection to Dirtyworks.ai

| Campaign object | Managed AI meaning |
|---|---|
| Luxury vessel | AI product or company account |
| Blank medallion | Product identity, composited only after approval |
| Orange power cord | The operating work that makes access useful |
| Endless receipt | Licences, consumption, overlap, support, and renewal cost |
| Marble plinth | Procurement and status given to the product |
| Tiny output | The actual job the company needed completed |
| Attendant/model | Buyer attraction and perceived sophistication |
| Product register/end card | Portfolio ownership and cost control |

Do not imply that the tiny output has no value. It is useful; its scale makes the operational excess visible.

- `AI SHOULDN'T BE A LUXURY.`

### Supporting lines

- `KEEP THE INTELLIGENCE. LOSE THE EXCESS.`
- `USEFUL DOESN'T HAVE TO MEAN EXTRAVAGANT.`
- `THE TOOL IS GOOD. THE OPERATING MODEL IS EXPENSIVE.`
- `BUY THE CAPABILITY. OPERATE THE COST.`
- `ANOTHER LICENCE IS NOT AN OPERATING MODEL.`
- `THE RENEWAL ROW IS WHERE THE MONEY IS.`

### Recommended claim discipline

Prefer statements about the buyer's operating model:

- unmanaged licences;
- overlapping products;
- abandoned access;
- unbounded consumption;
- unclear ownership;
- renewal without review;
- support and integration work that was never assigned.

Avoid unverified comparative claims such as `Product X costs too much`, `save 40%`, or `Vendor Y is a luxury`. Any price, saving, or vendor-specific statement requires a dated source, defined configuration, market/currency context, and approval before publication.

### End-card form

```

Alternative closer:

dirtyworks.ai
```

## Campaign architecture

### Tier 1 — Product films

The scalable recurring unit. One product vessel, one receipt behavior, one modest output, one campaign line. These should carry most of the series.

### Tier 2 — Product details

Two-to-six-second loops:

- orange cable clicks into the vessel;
- receipt ribbon begins, then keeps going;
- condensation or mist passes over the blank medallion;
- camera reveals the tiny output beside the monumental plinth;
- product rotates a few degrees under hard rim light.

### Tier 3 — Fashion/location films

Larger campaign beats featuring the canonical model and product in a remote location. Use sparingly so the campaign remains producible.

### Tier 4 — Evidence turn

The satire resolves into Dirtyworks.ai's operating language:

- product register;
- named owner;
- named users;
- cost known;
- renewal reviewed;
- overlap removed;
- exit retained.

This turn can happen on the landing page, in a carousel's final card, or after the video end card. Do not fill the luxury film itself with operational labels.

## Product-system bible

These are working production defaults. They may be changed before a series begins. Once locked, change them only by updating the canonical reference pack and this brief.

### `V-01` — canonical AI vessel

**Role:** A neutral luxury object onto which a product identity may be composited. It is not a real product, perfume, device, or package.

**Locked geometry:**

- rectangular obsidian-black glass body with softened corners;
- approximately 1.35 times taller than wide and 0.35 times as deep as wide;
- broad rounded shoulders, heavy glass base, no visible seam;
- short centered chrome neck and low hexagonal chrome cap;
- large centered circular matte-black medallion occupying approximately 42% of the front width;
- one narrow horizontal receipt slot centered at the bottom front edge;
- one small cable port low on the viewer-right side;
- no display, keyboard, controls, buttons, camera, vents, speaker, or recognizable perfume detail.

**Locked materials:**

- flawless near-black glass with subtle transparent depth at the edges;
- medallion is matte black, flat, and free of generated marks;
- cap is polished cool chrome, not gold;
- reflections are controlled and do not reveal a real studio or crew.

**Locked behavior:**

- the vessel remains inert and desirable;
- it does not speak, glow, pulse, float, transform, or behave like a robot;
- motion comes from camera, silk, mist, receipt, and cable;
- if a vendor identity is used, it is composited on the medallion in post.

### Orange cable

- Dirtyworks.ai signal orange, approximately `#FF5A1F`;
- thin, matte-rubber power/data cable;
- enters the vessel at the lower viewer-right side;
- appears as the only saturated color in most executions;
- never becomes a glowing energy conduit or supernatural effect;
- may remain unplugged in a `not operated` episode and connected in an `operated` resolution.

### Receipt ribbon

- fine warm-ivory paper, 55–70 mm wide;
- faint abstract mono marks, never legible prices, names, or account data;
- emerges from the vessel's lower front slot or from under the base;
- forms one elegant S-curve before continuing off-frame;
- must feel physically real: gravity, paper stiffness, friction, and wind remain consistent;
- quantity communicates excess; avoid dollar signs, raining cash, burning money, or cartoon piles.

### Output card

- warm-bone uncoated card, approximately 3 × 5 inches in the depicted scale;
- one modest output per execution;
- no generated body text; add any copy in post;
- placed beside the plinth, never hidden by the receipt;
- visually useful and complete rather than damaged or worthless.

### Marble plinth

- square white-to-cool-grey marble block;
- sharp edges, approximately the vessel's full height;
- restrained dark grey veining; no gold;
- monumental relative to the output card;
- same veining pattern or approved reference across product shots when continuity matters.

## Product identity and vendor-mark rules

Generated vendor marks are not acceptable production assets. They may be malformed, inaccurate, or visually imply an unauthorized relationship.

For any vendor-specific execution:

1. Obtain the current official mark from the vendor's approved brand resource.
2. Review current trademark, attribution, clear-space, color, and comparative-advertising rules.
3. Record the source URL, retrieval date, asset version, and allowed use.
4. Composite the mark onto the blank medallion after image/video generation.
5. Preserve the official mark exactly; do not restyle it as a perfume crest.
6. Add a disclosure where needed that vendors are examples and do not sponsor or endorse Dirtyworks.ai.
7. Verify every price or product claim immediately before publication.

Product names may be used descriptively only after legal/brand review. Until then, generate category executions such as `WORKFORCE ASSISTANT`, `RESEARCH`, `IMAGE`, `CODE`, or `AUTOMATION` as post-produced campaign labels—not marks on the generated vessel.

## Output matrix

The tiny output can vary by category while the vessel and set remain constant:

| Product category | Modest useful output | Cost/excess behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Workforce assistant | one concise meeting follow-up card | receipt loops around the entire plinth |
| Research assistant | one sourced memo card | multiple receipt layers accumulate before the memo appears |
| Image generation | one approved campaign frame lying flat | discarded receipt continues beyond the set |
| Coding assistant | one reviewed change card | receipt feeds while the vessel sits idle between prompts |
| Automation | one completed workflow card | three receipt ribbons imply overlapping tools; use carefully |
| Knowledge assistant | one supported answer card | receipt stops only when the orange operating cable connects |

Do not claim the depicted output is all a named product can do. It represents the bounded job the buyer actually needed from that instance.

## Talent bible

Product-only films do not require a person. When fashion/location executions use a model, keep one canonical adult character.

### `THE MUSE`

**Role:** Embodies desire, status, and the attraction of capability. She is not a vendor representative, customer, or Dirtyworks.ai employee.

**Locked appearance:**

- adult woman, approximately 30–36;
- tall, lean build and upright posture;
- medium olive skin;
- angular oval face, high cheekbones, straight dark eyebrows, dark eyes;
- long dark-brown hair arranged in a sculptural high bun with a few windblown strands;
- no celebrity resemblance;
- expression controlled, observant, and aloof; never seductive toward the product or camera.

**Locked wardrobe:**

- full-length black couture gown;
- high pleated neck, structured shoulders, narrow waist, long wind-responsive skirt;
- matte and semi-sheer fabric layers without visible skin beyond face and hands;
- black gloves optional but must remain consistent within an execution;
- no jewellery, logos, handbag, fur, feathers, or recognizable designer motifs.

**Performance behavior:**

- slow, economical movement;
- looks past or toward the vessel, rarely into camera;
- never smiles, gasps, worships the product, or behaves comically;
- the campaign's humour comes from scale and cost, not her performance.

## Location bible

### `STUDIO 01` — primary scalable set

- deep black void;
- wet black lacquer or stone floor with controlled reflection;
- one black silk panel entering from the viewer-left;
- light theatrical mist behind the vessel;
- white-grey marble plinth;
- no architecture, furniture, florals, chandeliers, or recognizable luxury props.

### `COAST 01` — flagship fashion location

- remote fictional black volcanic beach;
- wet reflective sand, distant dark cliffs, heavy sea mist;
- no recognizable landmark, settlement, road, or tourism feature;
- severe overcast or moonlit sky;
- receipt ribbon remains physically grounded and affected by wind;
- model and vessel remain the only deliberate objects.

### Optional future locations

- austere brutalist marble interior;
- remote salt flat under overcast sky;
- empty dark theatre stage;
- high-altitude snow field with black plinth.

Approve one location before generating a run. Do not mix unrelated luxury tropes within one clip.

## Visual bible

### Product photography

- 80–100 mm studio-lens behavior.
- Near-monochrome silver, black, marble white, and ivory.
- Hard sculpting rim lights with deep controlled blacks.
- Crisp material edges, restrained fine grain, premium magazine finish.
- Product is slightly above frame midpoint with negative space for copy.
- Receipt forms a deliberate foreground line; output card remains legible.

### Fashion/location photography

- 40–60 mm cinematic lens behavior; wide enough to reveal scale, not facial distortion.
- Severe monochrome, crushed but detailed blacks, silver highlights.
- Slow wind, fabric movement, mist, and wet reflections.
- Extreme negative space and off-centre placement.
- One orange cable is the only color unless a deliberate exception is recorded.

### What it must not become

- a counterfeit fragrance campaign;
- a recognizable luxury house's visual identity;
- gold-money cliché, yacht, private jet, champagne, jewellery, or supercar shorthand in every execution;
- generic sci-fi product render;
- cyberpunk, purple neon, glassmorphism, holograms, or visible robots;
- anti-technology satire;
- glamour imagery in which the model overwhelms the operating idea.

## Motion and audio bible

### Motion

- Slow, deliberate camera moves: 5–15 degree orbit, controlled push-in, macro slide, or locked frame.
- Product never deforms or changes geometry.
- Receipt advances with a small mechanical feed, then continues longer than expected.
- Silk and mist move independently at low speed.
- Fashion fabric movement may be captured as restrained slow motion.
- Avoid whip pans, handheld shake, fast zooms, glitch transitions, morphs, floating pieces, and impossible liquid simulations.

### Audio

- Deep controlled room tone or distant surf.
- Soft mechanical receipt feed is the recurring sonic signature.
- One cable click may mark the operating turn.
- Original minimal score: low sustained tone, isolated piano note, bowed metal, or silence.
- Avoid cash-register sounds, comic boings, recognizable luxury-ad soundalikes, breathy sexualized voice-over, and trailer impacts.
- Caption voice-over and meaningful mechanical sounds.

### Voice-over behavior

Use a calm, plain-spoken voice that contrasts with the image. Do not imitate fragrance-ad copy or whisper meaningless abstractions.

Example:

```text
The tool is useful.
The excess is optional.
```

## Video structure

### Six-to-ten-second product film

1. **Desire — 0–2 s:** rim light reveals the vessel.
2. **Capability — 2–4 s:** orange cable connects or output card appears.
3. **Excess — 4–7 s:** receipt continues feeding beyond expectation.
4. **Resolution — 7–10 s:** hard cut to campaign line and Dirtyworks.ai.

### Twelve-to-fifteen-second fashion film

1. Wide location and model reveal.
2. Slow move toward the vessel.
3. Receipt crosses the frame or horizon.
4. Cut to the tiny output.
5. End card states the operating message.

Do not attempt an entire 15-second story in one generation. Produce 2–5 second shots with controlled start/end frames, then assemble them in edit.

### 01 — The endless receipt

Locked product shot. The vessel is desirable. Receipt begins elegantly, then continues past the expected cut.

**End card:** `AI SHOULDN'T BE A LUXURY.`

### 02 — The one useful note

Macro shots imply enormous production value. Final cut reveals one useful warm-bone card beside the plinth.

**End card:** `USEFUL DOESN'T HAVE TO MEAN EXTRAVAGANT.`

### 03 — The overlap

Three identical vessels stand on separate plinths. Three receipt ribbons converge toward one identical output. Use category labels in post; do not add vendor marks until approved.

**End card:** `THREE TOOLS. ONE JOB.` / `OVERLAP HAS A COST.`

### 04 — The renewal

The vessel remains untouched under perfect light while the receipt advances. A calendar-page effect should be created in post, not generated with legible dates.

**End card:** `RENEWAL IS A DECISION.`

### 05 — Operated

The orange cable connects. The receipt stops. The useful output remains. This resolves the satire without suggesting the product should disappear.

**End card:** `KEEP THE INTELLIGENCE. LOSE THE EXCESS.`

### 06 — Flagship coast film

The Muse and vessel occupy the volcanic coast. Receipt crosses the beach toward the horizon. Cut to the output card, then Dirtyworks.ai.

**End card:** `THE TOOL IS GOOD. THE OPERATING MODEL IS EXPENSIVE.`

## Generation and continuity workflow

### Step 1 — Build the canonical reference pack

Approve before generating a series:

- `V-01` front, left three-quarter, right side, rear, top, and receipt-slot close-up;
- vessel on transparent background for controlled compositing if needed;
- exact medallion geometry and safe area;
- marble plinth front and three-quarter reference;
- receipt-paper material and S-curve reference;
- orange cable color/material reference;
- output card material and scale reference;
- Studio 01 empty set and lighting reference;
- The Muse neutral portrait, profile, full front, full side, and wardrobe turn;
- Coast 01 wide location plate.

Use the same approved reference set in every later generation. Written prompting alone will not preserve vessel geometry, model identity, fabric construction, or marble veining reliably.

### Step 2 — Separate generation from branding

1. Generate a clean unbranded vessel and scene.
2. Approve motion, lighting, geometry, receipt, and output.
3. Composite official vendor marks and campaign typography in post.
4. Verify mark integrity at every exported size.
5. Maintain an unbranded master so the scene remains reusable.

### Master prompt block

Copy unchanged into every luxury generation, then add one shot delta:

```text

PROP LOCK: warm-ivory receipt ribbon with unreadable abstract mono marks forms one physically plausible S-curve and exits frame; one small warm-bone output card remains visible; thin matte signal-orange cable is the only saturated color and enters low on viewer-right.

IMAGE LOCK: photorealistic luxury product/fashion photography; near-monochrome silver, black, marble white, and ivory; 80–100 mm product-lens behavior; hard sculpting rim lights; deep detailed blacks; controlled reflections; fine film grain; vast negative space; satire played completely straight.

CONSTRAINTS: original unbranded object; no generated vendor mark, readable price, invoice, UI, watermark, celebrity, recognizable luxury packaging, gold-money cliché, robot, neon, purple gradient, sexualized imagery, or comic performance.

SHOT DELTA: <one camera move, one receipt/cable/model action, duration, framing, and exact invariants>.
```

## Review checklist

Reject or repair a generated shot when any answer is no:

- Does V-01 match the approved silhouette, cap, medallion, receipt slot, and cable port?
- Is the medallion blank in the generation master?
- Is the receipt physically plausible and unreadable?
- Is the output visible and portrayed as useful?
- Is orange the only saturated color?
- Does the image feel expensive before the cost joke appears?
- Does the message criticize unmanaged excess rather than AI capability or a vendor?
- Are product identity and typography added only in post?
- If a vendor mark is used, is its source, version, permission basis, and clear space recorded?
- Are price and savings claims absent or fully sourced and dated?
- Is The Muse consistent with the approved identity and wardrobe?
- Does the execution remain original rather than resembling a specific luxury campaign?
- Can the video be captioned and described accessibly?

## Distribution and test boundary

Initial use:

- Dirtyworks.ai and founder social channels;
- short product loops, carousels, and campaign landing page;
- Notes article or product-register content that explains the operating mechanism;
- no site-wide photography replacement during the initial test.

Measure separately:

- visual engagement versus comprehension of the cost-control message;
- whether viewers think the campaign attacks AI generally or a named vendor;
- target-buyer profile visits, conversations, and campaign-assisted enquiries;
- recognition of licence overlap, ownership, consumption, or renewal as the actual issue;
- performance of product-only executions versus expensive fashion/location executions;
- production time required per approved vendor-specific variant.

Set numeric thresholds against the account's observed baseline before launch. Consider broader photography integration only after at least two creative cycles show sustained target-buyer response, correct message comprehension, and enough variation that the aesthetic can support more than one product joke.

## Accessibility and disclosure

- Caption voice-over and meaningful mechanical sounds.
- Do not rely on the orange cable alone to communicate `operated`; state the resolution in copy or alt text.
- Alt text should describe the luxury object, excessive receipt, modest output, and satirical intent.
- Identify fictional generated campaign scenes when necessary to prevent confusion.
- Never portray generated models as employees, customers, vendor representatives, or participants in a real Dirtyworks.ai engagement.
- Where named vendors appear, clarify that product names are descriptive examples and do not imply endorsement, resale authority, suitability, or fixed price.
