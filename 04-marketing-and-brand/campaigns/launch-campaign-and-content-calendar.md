## Purpose

This is the working 13-week promotion schedule. It translates the promotion strategy into founder actions, assets, distribution, conversations, gates, and reusable copy. Dates begin when the sponsor selects the first direct cohort; do not interpret “week 1” as a fixed calendar date.

## Operating assumptions

- founder capacity is approximately 20 hours per week;
- founder promotion/content capacity is capped near five hours per week, excluding live conversations and proposals;
- Calgary-area energy-service/vendor SMBs are the working first-cohort option, subject to sponsor selection and access threshold;
- the website may still be private during early weeks;
- the conversion is a relevant problem conversation, then a paid review only when qualified;
- all numerical activity targets are experiment assumptions, not market benchmarks.

## Campaign copy bank

These are starting structures, not scheduled final copy. Each post should be revised using real founder experience, cohort language, and evidence.

### Post 01 — The answer desk

> Every established company has an unofficial search engine.
>
> It is usually a person.
>
> They know which folder is current, which template is approved, which old project matters, and which exception was agreed three years ago.
>
> The obvious cost is interruption. The larger cost is that work waits, onboarding depends on access to one person, and two people can act from different answers.
>
> This is not automatically an AI problem. First, it is an ownership, source, permission and verification problem.
>
> I am researching where this shows up in established Alberta service companies. What was the last piece of work that waited for the person who knew where the answer lived?

### Post 02 — Mechanism

> “Put the documents in a chatbot” skips the hard questions.
>
> Which documents?
>
> Who says they are current?
>
> Who is allowed to see each answer?
>
> What happens when two sources disagree?
>
> Which questions should the system refuse?
>
> Who notices when the business changes?
>
> The interface is the visible part. The operating system behind it is sources, owners, access, tests, support and change.

### Post 03 — Tool register

> More AI licences are not an operating model.
>
> A company needs to know:
>
> - which products are approved;
> - who owns the account and contract;
> - which users and data are in scope;
> - what the product costs when usage changes;
> - how a user is removed;
> - how company work is exported or deleted;
> - who supports it when it fails.
>
> Buying is one event. Operating is the service.

### Post 04 — No-go trust

> A useful AI operator should sometimes recommend no AI launch.
>
> If no one owns the source material, access cannot be bounded, the outcome cannot be checked, or the first use would automate high-impact judgment, the responsible answer may be repair the process, choose a simpler tool, or stop.
>
> A no-go decision is not a failed review. It is evidence that someone did the work before the risk became production.

### Post 05 — Pilot gap

> A pilot proves that something can happen once.
>
> Production needs different evidence:
>
> - the right people see the right information;
> - supported answers point to approved sources;
> - unsupported questions fail safely;
> - users know when and how to verify;
> - someone owns support and incidents;
> - cost and vendor changes are visible;
> - the customer can exit with its work intact.
>
> Possibility earns a test. Operations earn continued use.

### Post 06 — Energy-services adaptation

> Energy-service companies do not only run on field expertise.
>
> Commercial teams, project coordinators and service managers also work across client requirements, proposal material, internal systems, prior jobs, closeout records and exceptions.
>
> The first useful AI scope is not an unsupervised safety or engineering decision. It may be the lower-risk administrative knowledge that keeps experienced people answering the same questions and slows everyone around them.
>
> That boundary is not a weakness. It is how a useful first system earns trust.

### Post 07 — MSP seam

> An MSP can own identity, tenant security, endpoints and the client relationship without wanting to own source quality, answer evaluations, user adoption and monthly knowledge improvement.
>
> An AI operations partner can own those things without pretending to be the MSP.
>
> The opportunity is not vague collaboration. It is a visible responsibility seam: who sells, approves, configures, supports, responds, reports and renews.

## Outreach structures

### Warm introduction request

> I am researching where established [energy-service/accounting/advisory] companies lose time because a current operating answer is hard to find or lives with one experienced person. I am speaking with owners and operations leaders about recent examples—not selling a broad AI transformation. Is there one person in your network who has dealt with that recently and might be open to a 25-minute working conversation?

### Referred first message

> [Referrer] suggested I contact you because of your role at [Company]. I am researching the last-mile operating work behind useful company AI: approved sources, users, support, quality and cost. In particular, I am looking at recent situations where work waited for the person who knew where a current answer lived. [Referrer] thought the question might be relevant to your experience. Would you be open to a 25-minute conversation about the last time this happened? I am happy to send the discussion questions first.

Before use, confirm the exact CASL basis and include required sender/contact and unsubscribe information. Where relying on the referral provision, follow the CRTC's specific conditions, including naming the referrer and limiting the exempt first message.

### Researched account message — energy service

> I noticed [specific growth, new branch, hiring, system, project, or leadership event] at [Company]. In service businesses, those changes often leave commercial and project staff searching across old jobs, client requirements, templates and internal systems before they can act. I am researching that operating problem separately from safety-critical or engineering decisions. Is this a problem you own or see, and would a short recent-event conversation be useful?

### Artifact follow-up

> You mentioned [specific event] and that [role/person] absorbs the questions. I turned the pattern into a one-page diagnostic covering frequency, wait time, sources, ownership and consequence. I am sending it because it relates directly to the event you described, not as a general mailing. If you mark what is wrong or missing, I will incorporate the correction into the anonymized research.

### Roundtable invitation

> I am convening 8–12 Calgary owners and operations leaders for a private working session: “What stops when the person who knows is away?” It is not an AI product demo. We will compare recent operating events, map where answers break, and discuss what is safe and useful to manage. I will return an anonymized pattern memo to the group. Would you like the one-page outline before deciding?

## Roundtable run of show

| Minute | Activity | Output |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–5 | purpose, confidentiality and no-sales boundary | shared rules |
| 5–15 | anonymized patterns from interviews | recognition/correction |
| 15–30 | recent-event exercise in pairs | event, role, wait, consequence |
| 30–42 | map source, owner, permission, contradiction and escalation | operating mechanism |
| 42–50 | what should remain human or out of scope | boundary evidence |
| 50–55 | explain the review and fit/non-fit | transparent commercial path |
| 55–60 | opt-in follow-up and pattern-memo permission | next actions and consent |

No attendee quote, logo, company name, recording, transcript, photograph, or attributed insight is public without explicit permission for that use.

## Paid-test gate

Run the first $1,500–$3,000 paid promotion only if all are true:

- selected cohort and buyer roles are stable enough to target;
- one organic post or artifact has created substantive engagement from at least three people in the target cohort, or has helped produce two relevant conversations;
- the landing page and qualification path match the promoted claim;
- analytics, privacy notice, consent and follow-up are functional;
- founder capacity exists to respond to interest within two business days;
- no claim depends on non-existent customer proof;
- there is a written stop rule and maximum spend.

Possible first formats remain open:

1. promote a founder point-of-view post to matched roles;
2. promote a private-roundtable application;
3. promote a practical register/diagnostic with qualification;
4. do not run paid media and use the budget for a second co-hosted operator session.

The choice depends on observed signal, not platform fashion.

## Intent-tool trial gate

A GojiBerry or similar trial is a prospect-research experiment, separate from the paid-media gate. Start only if all are true:

- cohort, company-size band, buyer roles, exclusions, and priority triggers are written;
- the vendor answers how LinkedIn-derived signals and actions are authorized, and the founder accepts the remaining account/terms risk;
- data-processing terms, subprocessors, security, deletion, opt-out, enrichment provenance, and Canadian use have been reviewed;
- no LinkedIn account is connected for automated actions under the current working policy;
- review mode and a maximum 25–50-account test are configured;
- every enriched contact still receives a recorded CASL/contact-basis decision before email;
- the manual comparison cohort uses the same ICP, period, and qualification definitions.

Measure research time, accounts accepted/rejected, false signal rate, relevant responses, conversations, qualifications, cash cost, and founder time. The trial succeeds only if it improves relevant conversations or materially reduces research time without weakening compliance, trust, or learning quality.

## End-of-cycle decisions

At week 13, the sponsor chooses among real options:

- repeat the same cohort and campaign because recognition and commercial conversion are emerging;
- narrow the cohort because one company type, trigger, buyer, or question domain dominates;
- move to the accounting alternative because access or willingness-to-pay is materially better;
- retain the direct cohort but change the entry campaign from answer-desk to tool/AI-operations management;
- emphasize the MSP route because it creates qualified customer access and workable economics;
- redesign the offer/message when conversations recognize the problem but reject the commercial step;
- pause promotion when there is no comparable problem evidence, no credible access, or no capacity to fulfil.

Record the evidence and decision in the company-building log. Do not silently roll an unsuccessful campaign into another three months.
