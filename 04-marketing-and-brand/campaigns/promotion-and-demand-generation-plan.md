# Dirtyworks.ai promotion and demand-generation plan

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**Status:** Ready for sponsor direction and first-cohort execution  
**Applies to:** founder-led direct market, MSP partner lane, website, events, editorial content, email, social, earned and paid promotion  
**Commercial conversion:** a relevant problem conversation followed, when qualified, by a paid review

## Executive direction

Dirtyworks.ai should not launch as a high-volume AI content brand. It should become recognizable among a small set of Alberta operators as the company that understands the difficult operating work behind useful workplace AI: product selection, accounts, approved sources, permissions, training, support, quality, incidents, and cost.

Promotion has three jobs:

1. make a costly but often unnamed operating problem recognizable;
2. make Dirtyworks.ai's method and judgment inspectable before a buyer takes a meeting;
3. create a credible path into a problem conversation or MSP design pilot.

The first 90 days are an evidence programme, not a scale campaign. The core motion is founder voice plus named-account distribution plus small operator conversations. Broad paid acquisition, large sponsorships, lead-list purchases, and an SEO content factory remain outside the first phase.

## Relationship to the go-to-market system

This document governs promotion: attention, recognition, trust, invitations, content distribution, events, and channel amplification. The [go-to-market strategy](../../05-sales-and-partnerships/sales-strategy/go-to-market-strategy.md) governs targeting, interviews, qualification, sales stages, proposals, and funnel outcomes.

```text
Observed operating problem
        ↓
Founder point of view / useful artifact / peer conversation
        ↓
Relevant account engages or accepts an introduction
        ↓
Problem conversation
        ↓
Qualified opportunity
        ↓
Paid Knowledge Reliability Review or agreed AI Operations Review
        ↓
Launch → managed operation → measured proof
```

Promotion is successful when it improves the quality and conversion of this path. Impressions, followers, clicks, downloads, and registrations are diagnostic signals, not business outcomes.

## Market focus

### Direct lane

Use one concentrated cohort at a time:

- working first option: Calgary-area energy-service/vendor SMBs, if the founder or MSP can reach ten credible conversations within six weeks;
- first alternative: independent accounting firms when access is materially stronger;
- later alternative: HR/advisory firms when a strong network path exists.

The first direct cohort must be chosen before segment-specific promotion is released. General founder point-of-view content may begin earlier, but paid targeting and vertical claims should not.

### MSP lane

Treat the existing MSP opportunity as a separate co-distribution experiment. The goal is one named-customer design pilot, not partner-signup volume. Lead with the operational practice the MSP can add, the responsibility seam, and the customer's retained technology/account ownership—not an unqualified white-label promise.

## Audience and buying group

### Primary economic buyer

- owner, managing partner, president, COO, general manager, or operations leader;
- accountable for service consistency, staff capacity, risk, and operating cost;
- interested in AI but unwilling to create an unmanaged tool estate or internal AI department.

### Operational champion

- operations/practice manager, office manager, project administrator, service coordinator, finance/IT lead, or long-tenured knowledge holder;
- repeatedly answers questions, repairs inconsistencies, onboards staff, or coordinates systems;
- needs relief without having their expertise treated as replaceable.

### Hidden or approval buyers

- IT/MSP, privacy/security, finance, legal/procurement, HR, and source owners;
- care about customer ownership, access, cost exposure, claims, responsibility, and exit;
- need content written for scrutiny, not merely enthusiasm.

This buying-group view matters because current Edelman–LinkedIn research reports that hidden buyers consume and use thought leadership in vendor evaluation at rates similar to target buyers, while buying-group misalignment stalls many B2B deals. The implication is not to create more content; it is to make each important assertion understandable to an operator, finance reviewer, and technical/risk reviewer.

## Message-to-market system

### Brand-level promise

> We do the work behind AI that works.

### Category explanation

> Managed knowledge and AI operations for Alberta businesses.

### Problem-level entry points

| Entry point | Buyer recognition | Dirtyworks.ai mechanism | Useful next action |
|---|---|---|---|
| The answer-desk tax | Work waits for one person who knows where the current answer lives | Bound the question domain, approved sources, ownership, verification, and measured use | Knowledge Reliability Review |
| The unmanaged AI estate | Staff adopt overlapping tools, licences, and accounts without a common operating model | Product register, selection, account/user administration, policy, cost and exit control | AI portfolio working session |
| The pilot-to-production gap | A demo works, but permissions, support, training, quality, and change are unowned | Managed launch, evaluation, runbooks, adoption, incidents, and monthly operation | Launch-readiness conversation |
| The MSP practice gap | The MSP owns infrastructure and security but not knowledge quality or user operating practice | Explicit partner seam plus Dirtyworks.ai method and recurring operation | One-customer design pilot |

### Message order

1. Start with a recognizable work event.
2. Name its operating cost or consequence.
3. Show the mechanism behind the problem.
4. State what Dirtyworks.ai manages.
5. State the boundary or non-fit.
6. Offer a useful conversation, diagnostic, or working session.

Do not lead with vendor names, model features, AI news, an exhaustive product catalogue, or a claim that every company needs AI.

## Campaign architecture

### Campaign 1 — `THE ANSWER DESK`

**Territory:** key-person dependence, interruptions, version uncertainty, onboarding, and slow answers.  
**Lead line:** “When one person becomes the company search engine, work waits.”  
**Anchor question:** “What stops when the person who knows is away?”  
**Primary asset:** a one-page Answer Desk Diagnostic.  
**Conversation:** 25-minute recent-event interview.  
**Event:** 8–12-person operator roundtable.  
**Commercial path:** Knowledge Reliability Review.

This is the strongest direct-market opening because it describes a business event without requiring the buyer to understand an AI category.

### Campaign 2 — `TOOL REGISTER / NO SURPRISES`

**Territory:** product sprawl, user access, renewals, shadow use, unclear ownership, overlapping spend, and supplier exit.  
**Lead line:** “More AI licences are not an operating model.”  
**Anchor question:** “Who knows which AI tools your company is paying for, who can access them, and what happens when someone leaves?”  
**Primary asset:** a blank, usable AI Product and Account Register.  
**Conversation:** 30-minute product-portfolio working session.  
**Commercial path:** product portfolio estimate plus management scope.

This campaign proves that the catalogue is one service layer rather than the whole offer.

### Campaign 3 — `PROOF BEFORE PILOT`

**Territory:** the work between a promising demo and dependable use.  
**Lead line:** “A pilot proves possibility. Operations prove whether it keeps working.”  
**Anchor question:** “Who owns sources, permissions, answer tests, support, cost, and exit after the launch meeting?”  
**Primary asset:** a sample AI Launch Control Register.  
**Conversation:** launch-readiness review.  
**Commercial path:** paid review or scoped launch after qualification.

### Campaign 4 — `PRACTICE / PARTNER`

**Territory:** the AI/knowledge operating layer for MSP clients.  
**Lead line:** “Keep the client relationship. Add the operating practice.”  
**Anchor question:** “Which part of AI delivery does the MSP own, and what happens after licences and tenant configuration?”  
**Primary asset:** one-page responsibility seam and partner-model comparison.  
**Conversation:** MSP discovery session followed by named-account mapping.  
**Commercial path:** one-customer referral, co-managed, or white-label design pilot.

## Channel roles

### 1. Founder profile — primary public voice

The founder's LinkedIn profile should be the main distribution surface during validation. The company page is the canonical brand record and asset library; it should not be expected to create initial trust by itself.

Publish from direct experience and current research:

- field notes from anonymized operator conversations;
- a clear contrarian point with its boundary;
- useful registers, checklists, and diagnostic questions;
- vendor-neutral explanations of how managed AI actually operates;
- lessons from designing the business, including valid no-go decisions.

Working cadence: two substantial founder posts per week and one deeper field note every two weeks. This is a capacity limit, not an algorithm prescription. Do not publish filler to maintain frequency.

### 2. Company LinkedIn page — validation and library

Use for:

- the offer/category explanation;
- launch announcements and event pages;
- canonical diagrams and work artifacts;
- selected founder-post resharing with added context;
- later customer-approved proof.

Working cadence: one or two posts per week. Avoid copying every founder post verbatim.

### 3. Website and Notes — durable proof

The website converts attention into comprehension and qualification. The Notes section stores the best durable material; it is not a keyword-volume blog.

Initial anchor notes:

1. “When one person becomes the company search engine.”
2. “More AI licences are not an operating model.”
3. “The work between an AI demo and dependable use.”
4. “What we will not automate first.”
5. “The responsibility seam between an MSP and an AI operations partner.”

Each note should contain an inspectable mechanism, a boundary, and one relevant next step. Ungated HTML is the default; offer a downloadable working artifact only when it is genuinely useful.

### 4. Warm introductions and named-account distribution

Content becomes promotion when it is deliberately placed in front of relevant people. For each anchor note or tool:

- send it personally to three to ten people for whom the problem is relevant;
- explain why it relates to their role or a prior conversation;
- ask for correction or a recent example, not a generic “thoughts?”;
- record the source, consent/relationship basis, message, response, and next action.

The first campaign uses 30 warm-introduction requests and a researched 50-account cohort, as defined in the GTM plan. Purchased lists and automated generic sequences are excluded.

### 5. Operator roundtables — trust and research

Run a small private session after approximately ten recent-event interviews. Suggested title:

> What stops when the person who knows is away?

Format:

- 60 minutes;
- 8–12 invited owners and operators;
- no product demo in the first 40 minutes;
- anonymized patterns, two working exercises, and peer discussion;
- final ten minutes: explain the paid review and invite fit conversations;
- publish an anonymized pattern memo afterwards with attendee permission rules stated in advance.

The first event should be hosted with a trusted introducer, MSP, customer peer, or relevant local organization when possible. LinkedIn supports public event pages for in-person or online events, but this first session should remain invitation-led even if a public event page is used for logistics.

### 6. Intent-signal prospecting tools — bounded research accelerator

Tools such as GojiBerry can reduce manual research by monitoring signals such as job changes, hiring, company growth, profile/content activity, and competitor engagement; scoring possible prospects; enriching business contact details; and drafting contextual outreach. This mechanism fits Dirtyworks.ai when it helps the founder decide **who deserves careful research now**. It does not fit when it turns the first cohort into high-volume autonomous outreach.

GojiBerry's current Pro offer advertises two AI agents, email and social outreach, enrichment, a unified inbox, and up to 1,800 contacted prospects per month for US$99/month. Its help material describes automated LinkedIn connection invitations, profile visits, messages, follow-ups, and email, with an optional review mode. Those capabilities are materially larger and more automated than the first 50-account experiment requires. [GojiBerry product/pricing](https://gojiberry.ai/) and [workflow glossary](https://help.gojiberry.ai/en/articles/12952573-gojiberry-glossary-key-terms)

The current working policy is:

- use an intent tool to discover and score potential trigger events, not to define the ICP;
- require a human to verify company fit, role, signal, source, outreach basis, and message before contact;
- use review/draft mode rather than autonomous sending;
- do not connect the founder's LinkedIn account or automate LinkedIn actions unless the vendor documents an authorized mechanism acceptable under LinkedIn's current terms;
- do not assume an enriched email or public professional activity establishes consent under CASL;
- preserve source, contact basis, suppression, message, response, and outcome in Dirtyworks.ai's own tracker;
- start with one 30-day, 25–50-account experiment and compare it with an equally sized manual cohort;
- cancel if the tool increases contact volume without improving relevant conversations or creates platform, privacy, trust, or data-quality concerns.

LinkedIn currently says third-party tools may not scrape its services or automate profile visits, connection requests, messages, posting, or engagement, and warns that member accounts using them risk restriction or shutdown. GojiBerry's own operational advice about gradually increasing connection volumes may reduce detection or activity spikes, but it does not change LinkedIn's stated permission boundary. [LinkedIn prohibited-software guidance](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387/prohibited-software-and-extensions%3Flang=en)

GojiBerry states that it processes public professional activity, LinkedIn URLs, business contact details, and third-party enrichment data, and places responsibility for lawful lead contact on its customer. Before any trial, review its data-processing terms, subprocessors, source provenance, deletion/opt-out handling, security, Canadian privacy applicability, and whether the proposed signal sources are contractually authorized. A GDPR “legitimate interest” position does not by itself establish a Canadian CASL consent basis. [GojiBerry privacy policy](https://gojiberry.ai/privacy-policy)

Three valid operating options remain:

| Option | Benefit | Constraint | Appropriate use now |
|---|---|---|---|
| GojiBerry bounded trial | Fast signal discovery, scoring, enrichment and message drafts | LinkedIn terms/provenance risk, data-controller duties, automation temptation, excessive default volume | 30-day research/review-mode comparison after vendor diligence |
| LinkedIn Sales Navigator plus manual tracker | Official LinkedIn research environment and high founder control | More manual time; weaker cross-channel automation | Lowest-platform-risk first cohort |
| Non-LinkedIn trigger research plus enrichment | Hiring pages, company news, leadership changes, local events and public business sources can create timely account hypotheses | More setup and fragmented signals | Useful when LinkedIn automation/provenance is unacceptable |

### 7. Local ecosystem and earned access

Use local organizations to gain relevant rooms and co-hosts, not merely logos. Current plausible ecosystems include:

- Calgary Chamber events, member workshops, and speaking opportunities;
- Platform Calgary community events and hosting;
- energy/service and consulting-engineering associations relevant to the selected cohort;
- Microsoft/Google/cloud user communities only when the subject remains operational rather than vendor promotional;
- accounting or HR associations only when those cohorts become active.

Priority order: attend and learn → secure a relevant introduction → co-host a working session → apply to speak → sponsor only with measured audience fit. Do not purchase a booth or broad sponsorship before a lower-cost participation test produces conversations.

### 8. Email briefing — owned relationship channel

Create an email briefing only when there is a real consented audience from conversations, events, customers, and explicit website opt-ins. Suggested name:

> `FIELD NOTE / AI OPERATIONS`

Cadence: monthly. Contents: one observed pattern, one operating tool, one boundary, one invitation. The email should point to durable website notes rather than become an isolated content stream.

Commercial electronic messages must follow CASL. Dirtyworks.ai must record express or applicable implied consent, identify the sender and contact information, include a working unsubscribe mechanism, action unsubscribes on time, and preserve evidence. A publicly visible business email is not blanket permission; relevance to the recipient's business role and other conditions must be assessed case by case. Obtain legal advice for the final outreach policy rather than treating this plan as a legal opinion.

### 9. MSP co-promotion

The first partner promotion sequence is operational:

1. partner discovery and qualification;
2. define customer/partner/Dirtyworks.ai responsibilities;
3. identify five plausible named customer accounts;
4. choose one problem campaign and one co-branded artifact;
5. run a private partner-client working session;
6. propose one paid design review;
7. measure customer access, conversion, effort, economics, and evidence rights.

Do not announce a partnership before the delivery, support, commercial, privacy, and customer-access seam is agreed.

### 10. Paid promotion — conditional amplifier

Paid media should amplify a message that already creates relevant conversations. It should not be used to discover the category from scratch.

First paid test, only after the evidence gate:

- format: founder thought-leadership promotion or a tightly targeted event/diagnostic campaign;
- audience: selected geography, industry, company size, and buyer roles;
- spend: $1,500–$3,000 CAD over two to four weeks;
- creative: one proven organic point of view and one useful asset;
- conversion: relevant problem conversation or roundtable application;
- stop rule: stop or redesign when spend produces attention but no target-account engagement, or when target engagement does not progress to conversations.

Search ads are a later test. Activate only when interviews reveal stable language buyers actually search and the website has a matching page. Retargeting is unnecessary until relevant traffic is large enough to create a meaningful audience and the consent/privacy implementation is approved.

## Content operating system

### Source hierarchy

1. anonymized recent-event interviews;
2. delivery evidence and approved customer outcomes;
3. Dirtyworks.ai operating artifacts and experiments;
4. primary regulatory, vendor, and research sources;
5. founder experience, clearly framed as experience or judgment.

Do not convert a single conversation into a market-wide claim. Label patterns by sample and date until evidence becomes stronger.

### One-source, many-uses loop

```text
Conversation or operating experiment
        ↓
Evidence note with claim boundary
        ↓
Founder post + short diagram + account-specific send
        ↓
Anchor article or working artifact
        ↓
Roundtable discussion
        ↓
Anonymized pattern memo
        ↓
Sales and offer refinement
```

### Editorial pillars

| Pillar | Question answered | Typical artifact |
|---|---|---|
| Work waits here | Where does knowledge friction become operating delay? | event story, diagnostic, interruption map |
| The operating layer | What must someone own after the AI product is selected? | lifecycle diagram, responsibility register |
| Product discipline | Which tools, accounts, permissions, costs, and exits exist? | product/account register, portfolio example |
| Proof and boundaries | How do we test usefulness without pretending certainty? | evaluation card, no-go example, claim register |
| People using the system | How do onboarding, support, and adoption actually work? | training pattern, support runbook, role story |
| Local operator field notes | What are Alberta SMB operators seeing now? | anonymized cohort pattern memo |

### Content acceptance test

Publish only when the item can answer yes to at least four of the first five questions and all three trust questions:

1. Does it describe a recognizable event or decision?
2. Does it expose a useful mechanism?
3. Does it contain a concrete artifact, question, or action?
4. Is there a point of view someone could disagree with?
5. Is it relevant to a named buyer group?
6. Are evidence, inference, and opinion distinguishable?
7. Are limitations and excluded uses stated where material?
8. Would the general impression remain accurate without relying on fine print?

### Proof states

| State | Allowed material | Prohibited implication |
|---|---|---|
| Before customers | method, founder experience, research, prototypes, sample artifacts, explicit hypotheses | customer outcomes, adoption, ROI, partner authorization, vendor resale rights |
| After interviews | dated and anonymized patterns with sample size | market-wide prevalence or willingness to pay |
| After paid reviews | review findings and decision quality with permission | production outcome or recurring reliability |
| After launch | measured acceptance, use, quality, and limitations with approval | causation beyond the measurement design |

Canadian advertising rules apply to the general impression of the work, including website visuals, social content, metadata, testimonials, and performance statements. Quantified performance claims require adequate supporting tests before publication. Generated photography is editorial imagery and must never imply a real employee, client, endorsement, or completed engagement.

## First 90-day programme

The detailed weekly schedule and sample campaign copy are in the [launch campaign and content calendar](launch-campaign-and-content-calendar.md).

### Phase 0 — operating foundation, weeks 1–2

- choose the first direct cohort;
- establish the 50-account list and 30 warm-introduction paths;
- configure the minimum tracker and consent evidence;
- finalize founder/company profiles and four conversion artifacts;
- publish one general point-of-view note;
- complete MSP discovery scheduling.

**Gate:** 30 plausible accounts, ten credible conversation paths, functional capture/qualification, and no unsupported public claims.

### Phase 1 — recognition and conversations, weeks 3–6

- release `THE ANSWER DESK` through founder posts, direct sends, and recent-event interviews;
- send two small, researched outreach waves rather than one mass sequence;
- capture language, objections, trigger events, and brand reactions;
- begin partner account mapping;
- invite the first roundtable only after recurring patterns exist.

**Gate:** ten relevant direct conversations, five independently recognized similar problems, at least three qualified opportunities or a clear record of why not, plus a qualified MSP decision path.

### Phase 2 — authority and conversion, weeks 7–10

- publish a dated, anonymized pattern memo;
- host the first private operator roundtable;
- offer paid reviews only to qualified accounts;
- release `PROOF BEFORE PILOT` or `TOOL REGISTER / NO SURPRISES` based on the strongest observed entry point;
- run the first partner-client working session if responsibilities are sufficiently defined.

**Gate:** three to five review proposals, evidence of willingness to pay, and at least one message/artifact producing relevant engagement beyond the founder's immediate supporters.

### Phase 3 — controlled amplification, weeks 11–13

- compare channel and campaign conversion;
- document loss mechanisms and cost in founder time;
- repeat the winning roundtable or asset;
- run a small paid amplification test only if the paid-media gate passes;
- choose whether cohort 2 stays vertical, moves to accounting, or narrows further.

**Gate:** one repeatable promotion-to-conversation path and a reasoned continue, narrow, redesign, or stop decision. A sale is desirable but not the only valid learning outcome; continued spending without qualified signal is not acceptable.

## Measurement system

### Business metrics

| Level | Measure | Definition | 90-day working hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Targeting | Researched accounts | account passes cohort definition and has buyer/trigger hypothesis | 50 |
| Access | Warm introduction requests | specific request made to a credible connector | 30 |
| Engagement | Target-account engagement | relevant reply, artifact discussion, event acceptance, or substantive interaction from an in-cohort person | establish baseline |
| Conversation | Relevant problem conversations | recent event discussed with a plausible buyer/champion | 20 across the original GTM period; at least 10 in cohort 1 |
| Recognition | Independent problem recognition | similar costly event described without being taught the category | 5 of first 20 |
| Qualification | Qualified opportunities | qualification threshold met | 10 hypothesis |
| Commercial | Paid reviews | paid review signed and scheduled | 3–5 hypothesis |
| Channel | Named MSP pilot | partner produces a qualified named customer buying a review | 1 |

These are hypotheses from the GTM system, not industry benchmarks or public promises.

### Promotion diagnostics

- content source and theme;
- named accounts reached and engaged;
- founder versus company distribution;
- warm, direct, partner, event, organic, paid, or other source;
- invitations, registrations, attendance, conversations, qualified follow-ups;
- cost in cash and founder/contractor hours;
- audience-role quality;
- objections, confusion, and claim/trust concerns;
- first touch, most influential touch, last touch, and buyer-reported source;
- next action and loss/no-action reason.

Do not optimize click-through rate in isolation. A post with modest reach and two relevant operator conversations can outperform a viral post that reaches no plausible buyers.

### Weekly decision questions

1. Which operating event was recognized without explanation?
2. Which audience role engaged, and was it inside the cohort?
3. Which artifact or point of view helped a conversation progress?
4. Where did trust, urgency, offer, price, or responsibility fail?
5. Did promotion create a conversation, or merely content activity?
6. What should be repeated, narrowed, revised, or stopped next week?

## Attribution mechanism

Record four fields rather than force a single-source answer:

- **first known touch:** how the account first encountered Dirtyworks.ai;
- **most influential touch:** what the buyer says changed their understanding or confidence;
- **conversion touch:** what led to the meeting, event, or proposal;
- **assist:** partner, person, content, or event that materially helped.

Ask on the Start form and in the first meeting: “What prompted you to speak with us now?” Preserve the buyer's words. Early attribution is qualitative because the volume is too small for modelled precision.

## Compliance and trust controls

- maintain a contact-source and consent/relationship field for every person receiving commercial email;
- include accurate sender identity, mailing/contact information, and working unsubscribe in applicable CEMs;
- keep a suppression list and process unsubscribes no later than the legal requirement;
- never scrape and bulk-message public emails merely because they are visible;
- obtain permission before naming a person, customer, MSP, event attendee, or vendor relationship;
- distinguish reseller authorization, referral, administration, and customer-direct billing;
- substantiate performance claims before publication and retain the test/evidence record;
- label samples, prototypes, generated people, estimates, and hypotheses accurately;
- do not put customer or interview details into public AI systems without permission and an approved data path;
- use a professional legal review before the first scaled outbound/email programme or public customer claim.

## Decisions preserved for the sponsor

1. Choose cohort 1: energy-service/vendor, accounting, or HR/advisory.
2. Choose the public commercial naming used in campaign calls to action: Knowledge Reliability Review, AI Operations Review, or an umbrella plus module.
3. Choose the website release scope and whether the campaign routes to a public Start page or a private invitation page first.
4. Choose the first budget gate and whether founder time or contractor support produces the campaign artifacts.
5. Choose whether the first roundtable is founder-hosted, MSP-co-hosted, or ecosystem-co-hosted.
6. Decide whether an email briefing begins in the first 90 days or only after the first roundtable creates a consented audience.

None of these choices prevents general field-note production, account research, MSP discovery, or drafting the campaign artifacts.

### Inferences

- Founder-led insight is likely more credible than company-page publishing during an unknown-brand launch, but its conversion must be measured in this market.
- Small, role-specific conversations should create stronger evidence and trust than a broad public AI webinar for the current offer.
- Local ecosystems can improve access, but paid membership or sponsorship has no assumed return until the audience and conversation path are tested.
- Paid media can amplify recognition after a message works; it cannot compensate for an unclear problem, weak trust, or unqualified offer.

### Constraints

- The direct subsegment has not been selected.
- There is no customer proof, consented audience, organic baseline, or channel conversion data yet.
- The founder is half-time and cannot sustain high-frequency publishing, broad outbound, events, partner development, selling, and delivery simultaneously.
- Website production is in progress, so early promotion must be able to route through private conversations and temporary assets without waiting for a full site.
- This plan provides operational safeguards but is not legal advice.

## Review cadence

- **Weekly:** promotion activity, target-account engagement, conversations, objections, cost/time, next experiment.
- **After ten cohort conversations:** message, campaign, roundtable, and segment decision.
- **After first roundtable:** attendance quality, conversation conversion, consented audience, repeat decision.
- **After first three review proposals:** offer/price/trust/language assessment.
- **After first paid review:** revise content and promotion using real approved evidence, without overstating outcomes.
- **Quarterly:** continue, narrow, expand, redesign, or stop each channel and campaign.
