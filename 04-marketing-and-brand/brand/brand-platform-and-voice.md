## The brand in one page

### What we believe

Useful AI is not produced by a better demo. It is earned through the work behind the interface: choosing trustworthy sources, assigning owners, fixing contradictions, setting permissions, testing answers, training people, handling failures, and improving the system as the company changes.

### What we do

Dirtyworks.ai gives teams reliable answers from approved company information and operates the knowledge, controls, support, and improvements that keep those answers useful.

Established Alberta businesses with enough shared knowledge and operating complexity to feel the cost of searching, interrupting, recreating, and relying on a few experienced people. Initial lanes are professional-services firms, energy-service companies, and traditional MSP partners serving similar clients.

### What we are against

AI theatre: broad promises, impressive demos, ownerless sources, invisible risk, unmanaged tools, and automation built before the work is understood.

## Voice principles

### 1. Start where work gets stuck

Lead with a recognizable event rather than a category.

**Use:** “The answer exists. Your team still cannot find it.”  
**Avoid:** “Organizations face growing knowledge-management challenges.”

### 2. Show the work behind the promise

Every confident claim should expose its mechanism.

**Use:** “We connect approved sources, test permissions, evaluate real questions, and keep the system current.”  
**Avoid:** “Our platform delivers seamless, trustworthy intelligence.”

### 3. Name the boundary

Restraint creates credibility. Say what remains human, excluded, or unproven.

**Use:** “It can surface an approved procedure. It does not replace the person accountable for the decision.”  
**Avoid:** “AI handles the process end to end.”

### 4. Prefer verbs and concrete nouns

Use find, verify, own, test, fix, connect, operate, and improve. Avoid noun piles such as enablement, transformation, optimization, modernization, orchestration, and intelligence layer unless the exact meaning follows.

### 5. Respect the people doing the work

Experienced employees are not bottlenecks to eliminate. They are carrying an invisible system the company has failed to support.

**Use:** “Stop making your most experienced people act as the company search engine.”  
**Avoid:** “Remove human bottlenecks.”

### 6. Earn confidence; do not perform certainty

Say what is observed, inferred, tested, and still open. Use strong declarative language for the method and calibrated language for expected outcomes.

**Use:** “We establish the baseline, then measure whether answer time changes.”  
**Avoid:** “Save 30% immediately.”

## Voice mechanics

### Sentence behavior

- Headlines: 2–10 words, declarative, often with a hard stop.
- Openings: one recognizable event or contradiction.
- Body copy: short paragraphs with one mechanism each.
- Lists: evidence, responsibilities, or decisions—not feature dumps.
- Questions: pointed and diagnostic, never vague engagement bait.
- Punctuation: periods, colons, slashes, brackets, and occasional em dashes. Avoid strings of exclamation marks.
- Contractions: use them naturally.
- First person: “we” when taking responsibility; “Dirtyworks.ai” when the brand must be explicit.
- Second person: “your team” and “your company,” not generic “users” when speaking to buyers.

### Capitalization

- Brand: `Dirtyworks.ai` in prose; lowercase `dirtyworks.ai` may be used in the wordmark.
- Offer names: title case when contractual; sentence case in conversational copy.
- Labels and proof marks may use uppercase: `SOURCE`, `OWNER`, `VERIFIED`, `OPEN GAP`, `DO NOT AUTOMATE`.
- Avoid title case for every website heading.

### Rhythm

Use contrast:

> The demo was the easy part.  
> The sources, permissions, ownership, testing, training, support, and change never were.

Use fragments deliberately, not constantly:

> Find it. Verify it. Use it.

Use a long sentence when the accumulation is the point, then stop:

> A useful answer depends on the current source, the right access, a question the system understands, evidence a person can inspect, and someone accountable when any of that changes. That is the work.

## Vocabulary

### Words we own

work, works, managed, approved, source, evidence, answer, current, owner, permission, verify, reliable, gap, repair, operate, accountable, improve, change, useful, company knowledge, human decision, named responsibility.

### Words used carefully

- **AI-powered:** say what AI does instead.
- **Secure:** name the control; never imply absolute security.
- **Accurate:** define the evaluated question class and limit.
- **Automation:** describe action, approval, exception, and rollback.
- **Agent:** use only when the buyer needs the implementation category.
- **Single source of truth:** use only when a true authoritative system exists.
- **ROI:** show baseline, method, period, and attribution.
- **Best practice:** name the source or practical control.

### Words we reject

revolutionary, game-changing, unleash, unlock, harness, seamless, frictionless, magic, supercharge, transformative, cutting-edge, future-proof, autonomous workforce, replace employees, eliminate hallucinations, one-click, set-and-forget.

## Message architecture

### Level 1 — Interruption

Make the buyer recognize the problem.

- Your company knows more than it can find.
- The answer exists. The work still waits.
- “Ask the person who knows” is not a knowledge system.
- Your best people should not be the company search bar.

### Level 2 — Outcome

- Reliable answers from approved company information.
- Less searching. Less asking. More work moving.
- Find the current answer. See the source. Know who owns it.

### Level 3 — Managed difference

- The AI is only the visible part.
- We operate the sources, permissions, quality, support, and change behind it.
- A system that works on launch day is a project. A system that keeps working is an operation.

### Level 4 — Trust

- Customer-owned technology and data by default.
- Visible sources and honest “not found.”
- Human accountability for consequential decisions.
- Defined scope, access, incidents, and offboarding.

### Level 5 — Action

- Show us where work gets stuck.
- Start with one question domain.
- Map the answers your team keeps chasing.
- Book a Knowledge Reliability Review.

## Audience adaptations

### Owner / COO / managing partner

Lead with continuity, elapsed time, consistency, and responsibility. Avoid architecture until asked.

> Your people are already paying for scattered knowledge—in interruptions, waiting, rework, and dependence on whoever remembers. We start with one costly question domain and determine whether it is worth fixing.

### Knowledge holder / experienced employee

Show respect and reduce surveillance concern.

> This is not an attempt to extract everything you know or measure who asks the wrong questions. It is a way to make approved answers easier to use and make missing or contradictory information visible to the people who own it.

### IT / security / MSP

Lead with responsibility seams, ownership, access, evaluation, incidents, and portability.

> You keep the tenant and security relationship. Dirtyworks.ai operates the approved knowledge use case. We write down who owns every source, permission, test, support tier, incident, and change before production.

### Professional services

> Make firm methods, templates, software procedures, and engagement administration easier to find—without putting client records or professional judgment on autopilot.

### Energy services

> Make commercial and project knowledge easier to use across teams while safety, engineering, field, and regulatory decisions stay with accountable people.

## Claims policy

Every external claim must be one of:

1. **Method claim:** what Dirtyworks.ai does and controls.
2. **Capability claim:** what the selected platform/configuration can do in a defined scope.
3. **Measured outcome:** customer result with baseline, period, method, and approval.
4. **Customer statement:** attributed and approved quotation.
5. **Market evidence:** sourced, dated, and not presented as customer proof.

Do not publish generalized savings, accuracy, security, compliance, or productivity claims without evidence and qualification.

## Voice quality check

Before publishing, ask:

- Does the first sentence name a recognizable event, result, or conflict?
- Did we expose the mechanism behind the claim?
- Did we respect the people currently carrying the work?
- Did we name a relevant boundary or limitation?
- Are abstract nouns replaceable with a verb or concrete thing?
- Could any other AI consultancy say this unchanged?
- Is the next action specific?
- Would we still say this during an incident or contract review?

If the last two answers are no, revise it.
