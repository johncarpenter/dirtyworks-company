## Brand thesis

Dirtyworks.ai does the difficult operational work that determines whether business AI remains useful after a demo: finding and repairing sources, mapping ownership and access, configuring systems, evaluating answers, training users, handling failures, and continuously improving the service.

The name works when “dirty work” means the necessary, unglamorous work behind a dependable result. It fails if the market hears low quality, reckless data handling, or a novelty AI agency. The visual identity, writing, controls, and proof must therefore feel precise and trustworthy rather than playful or messy.

## Recommended message system

### Brand line

> **We do the work behind AI that works.**

This preserves the founder’s thought—“we do the dirty work for AI while you do your work”—but removes repetition and avoids suggesting that customers can disengage from source truth, approvals, and adoption.

### Homepage problem line

> **Your company knows more than it can find.**

### Homepage explanation

> Dirtyworks.ai gives your team reliable answers from approved company information. We set up and operate the sources, permissions, quality checks, support, and improvements—using business technology you control.

### Category descriptor

> **Managed knowledge and AI operations for Alberta businesses.**

Use the descriptor near the brand until the market understands what the company does. Do not rely on the name or tagline alone.

## Alternative lines to test

| Line | Strength | Risk | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|
| We do the work behind AI that works. | Memorable, credible operating mechanism | Broad without explanation | Brand line |
| The dirty work behind dependable AI. | Strongest name connection | “Dirty” next to “AI” may weaken trust | Campaign or founder story |
| Your company knowledge, made useful. | Clear outcome | Understates managed operations | Offer page |
| AI that works the way your company works. | Aspirational and business-specific | Can overpromise adaptation/accuracy | Test in interviews |
| Reliable answers from the company information you already have. | Concrete and verifiable | Long; not a brand line | Offer promise |
| Your AI operations team—without building one. | Strong managed-service value | Leads with category rather than first pain | Expansion/MSP material |

## Audience-specific message

### SMB owner or operations leader

> Give staff a faster, verifiable path to the company answers they need—without building an internal AI team or adding another unmanaged tool.

### Professional-services firm

> Make approved methods, templates, engagement procedures, and firm knowledge easier to find and keep current—without putting client or employee decisions on autopilot.

### Energy-service company

> Make commercial, project, and service knowledge easier to use across teams while keeping high-impact operating and safety decisions under accountable human control.

### MSP partner

> Add a managed knowledge and AI operations practice to your client offering without building the delivery capability from scratch. Keep the account relationship; Dirtyworks.ai supplies the method, launch, quality, and ongoing operations behind it.

## Brand voice

- Plain-spoken, operational, and commercially literate.
- Confident about the method; precise about limitations.
- Technical when technical detail changes a decision, not to display sophistication.
- Respectful of existing staff, systems, and MSP relationships.
- Evidence-led: recent events, sources, owners, controls, and measured results.
- Willing to recommend a simpler non-AI solution or no launch.

Avoid hype, futurism, synthetic robot imagery, “magic,” “revolutionary,” “autonomous workforce,” “replace employees,” or messy/grunge visual clichés.

## Brand trust architecture

The name earns trust through visible operating proof:

- customer-owned technology and data by default;
- approved sources and visible references;
- least-privilege access and permission testing;
- honest abstention and human accountability;
- named source owners and change records;
- privacy, security, incident, and offboarding processes;
- explicit service boundaries;
- case studies with a real baseline and limitations;
- professional contracts, insurance, and specialist review.

## Brand validation questions

Do not ask only “Do you like the name?” Test mechanism and trust:

- Before I explain it, what would you expect a company named Dirtyworks.ai to do?
- What does the name make you trust or distrust?
- Does “We do the work behind AI that works” clarify the name?
- Which descriptor is clearest: managed knowledge operations, AI managed services, or an outsourced AI operations team?
- Would the name create concern when discussing company documents or permissions? Why?
- Would “powered by Dirtyworks.ai” strengthen or weaken an MSP’s offer?

### Pass threshold

Keep the brand—which is the current decision—unless research reveals a material sales or trust problem:

- at least 70% of relevant interviewees understand the operational/hard-work association after the descriptor;
- fewer than 20% express an unresolved security, quality, or professionalism objection;
- no recurring confusion with unrelated/adult/industrial services that impairs buyer willingness;
- the live MSP can plausibly use Dirtyworks.ai in co-branded material even if full white-label remains available.

If the brand fails, the domain can still serve as a campaign or holding brand while the customer-facing service takes a different name. That is an available fallback, not the current plan.

## Naming architecture

Use simple descriptive offers:

- **Dirtyworks.ai Knowledge Reliability Review**
- **Dirtyworks.ai Managed Knowledge Launch**
- **Dirtyworks.ai Knowledge Operations**
- **Dirtyworks.ai Managed Workflow**

For MSP white-label delivery, use generic offer names in the customer contract and retain “Delivered by Dirtyworks.ai” or subcontractor disclosure wherever contract, privacy, platform, or operational transparency requires it.
