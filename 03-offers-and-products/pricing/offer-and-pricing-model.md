# Offer and pricing model

**Version:** 0.2  
**Date:** 2026-08-26  
**Status:** Testable commercial model; not a published rate card

## Pricing principle

Dirty Works charges for a managed operating scope, not access to a model. Price is driven by the number and complexity of answer domains, source systems, permission groups, users, managed workflows, service level, risk, and expected change—not token consumption alone.

All prices below are CAD before tax. Customer-owned software licences, cloud use, travel, legal/privacy specialists, and unusual data remediation are separate unless explicitly included.

## Launch offer hierarchy — sponsor direction

The repeatable launch core is **Managed AI SaaS Operations** for packaged workforce products such as ChatGPT Enterprise and comparable AI SaaS. The [core operating model](../../06-client-delivery/service-design/core-managed-ai-saas-operating-model.md) defines the delivery and systems boundary.

The core includes product/procurement coordination, tenant administration, joiner/mover/leaver work, support, onboarding and training, adoption review, cost/seat/budget/renewal controls, a governance baseline, vendor escalation, reporting and offboarding. Its commercial structure is:

```text
One-time onboarding fee
+ monthly managed-platform fee
+ managed-user or managed-product quantity component
+ optional training/support capacity
+ vendor licences/usage shown separately unless authorized resale applies
```

The working `Register / Manage / Operate` amounts in the [managed AI product catalogue](../product-catalog/managed-ai-product-catalog.md) remain pricing hypotheses. They must be tested against actual support/admin time and the target gross-margin floor before becoming a public rate card.

Billing has three real options:

1. **Customer-direct default:** vendors invoice the customer; Dirtyworks.ai invoices its management service and supplies a consolidated cost statement.
2. **Authorized resale:** a single invoice is possible only for products/geographies covered by a recorded vendor or distributor authorization.
3. **Approved pass-through exception:** Dirtyworks.ai pays and rebills a defined charge only with pricing and contract protection for credit, cash, tax, foreign exchange, usage, cancellation and vendor-change risk.

A unified cost statement is part of the operating service. Do not promise a single unified invoice until seller-of-record rights and economics are confirmed.

Custom knowledge/data systems, retrieval, hosted models, production APIs, bespoke agents, integrations and application observability are **Advanced AI Workloads**. They require paid discovery, project scope and acceptance criteria, plus a separately priced recurring operating layer. They must not be silently included in the base user-management package.

The knowledge-specific offers below remain useful modules and validation packages. They no longer define the whole company or the minimum core service.

## Offer 1 — Knowledge Reliability Review

**Purpose:** decide whether a bounded managed-knowledge launch is feasible, valuable, and responsible.

**Target price:** $2,500 fixed. Test a $1,500–$3,500 range only through controlled pricing experiments; do not negotiate by silently expanding scope.

**Timing:** 7–10 business days from access to required people and information.

**Included:**

- executive and knowledge-owner interviews;
- one operating-question domain;
- inventory of up to three candidate source systems;
- sample of up to 25 representative files/items, inspected with customer permission;
- recent-event and baseline worksheet;
- source ownership, freshness, contradiction, permission, privacy, and risk screen;
- 15–30 real evaluation questions;
- recommended launch architecture, scope, price, risks, responsibilities, and no-go findings;
- executive readout and written Knowledge Reliability Map.

**Not included:** production connectors, full data audit, legal opinion, privacy impact assessment, document migration, employee-wide survey, or custom prototype.

**Exit outcomes:** proceed, remediate before launch, capture undocumented knowledge as a separate project, use a simpler non-AI solution, or stop. A review is successful when it enables a sound decision, even if that decision is not to implement.

**Commercial rule:** the assessment is paid in advance. Up to $1,250 may be credited against a Managed Knowledge Launch signed within 30 days. A partial credit preserves the value of diagnosis and avoids turning it into free presales.

## Offer 2 — Managed Knowledge Launch

**Purpose:** put one bounded answer domain into production and establish evidence of reliability, adoption, and operating value.

**Target price:** $9,500 fixed for the reference scope. Expected range is $7,500–$15,000 after a paid review.

**Reference scope:**

- one business unit or role group;
- up to 40 users;
- one answer domain;
- up to three supported source systems;
- up to four permission groups;
- a customer-owned platform selected during the review;
- source connection and approved content configuration;
- evaluation set, permission tests, abstention and escalation path;
- administrator and user training;
- baseline and adoption measurement;
- 30 days of stabilization;
- launch report and recurring-service plan.

**Change triggers:** more sources, custom connector development, broad document cleanup, interviews to capture undocumented knowledge, complex identity work, sensitive personal information, multilingual evaluation, after-hours cutover, or authoritative/safety-critical use.

**Payment:** 50% at scheduling, 40% at configured acceptance environment, and 10% at production handoff. Customer-caused access delays move dates but do not defer completed milestone invoices.

**Acceptance tests:**

- 100% of critical permission tests pass;
- agreed evaluation threshold is met by question class, not merely as one average;
- sources are visible for supported answers;
- unsupported/contradictory questions follow the agreed failure path;
- customer owner signs off source scope and user access;
- telemetry, feedback, incident, and offboarding paths are demonstrated;
- users and administrators receive the agreed training.

No launch promises perfect answer accuracy. High-impact instructions require source verification and human accountability.

### Recurring work included

- user, connector, source, and configuration administration within the managed scope;
- scheduled quality and permission regression tests;
- source freshness and owner follow-up;
- supported-user questions and incident triage;
- usage, failure, feedback, cost, and value reporting;
- vendor-change review;
- runbook, inventory, decision, and change-record maintenance;
- improvement work within the package allowance.

### Recurring work excluded

- general IT support or endpoint administration;
- unlimited prompt writing, integrations, content cleanup, or development;
- ownership of customer policies or source truth;
- legal, security-certification, employment, tax, accounting, engineering, or safety judgment;
- production actions outside documented workflow controls;
- third-party downtime guarantees;
- work outside the agreed support window.

Unused improvement capacity does not roll over. This is a scheduling boundary, not a bank of prepaid development hours. Work beyond it uses a change order or a larger package.

## Expansion offers

Expansion follows evidence from managed use:

1. **Knowledge repair sprint:** reconcile or author missing/stale source material with customer approval.
2. **New domain launch:** repeat the launch process for a new role group or question domain.
3. **Integration:** make an approved system of record available to the knowledge layer.
4. **Assisted workflow:** draft, summarize, classify, or recommend while a person remains accountable.
5. **Managed automation:** act on systems within approved inputs, controls, exceptions, and rollback paths.

Each expansion has its own business case, risk classification, evaluation/acceptance tests, and ongoing operating cost. Repetition alone does not justify automation.

## Internal pricing formula

Use the public packages as anchors, then validate scope internally:

```text
Monthly price floor =
  (forecast fully loaded delivery cost
   + dedicated platform/contractor cost
   + risk reserve)
  / (1 - target gross margin)
```

For a 60% target gross margin, a $2,500 package can carry at most $1,000 of steady-state monthly delivery and direct platform cost. The delivery plan should keep Core near 6–8 hours per month after stabilization. If it cannot, change the scope, standardize the work, or raise the price.

### Complexity adjustment checklist

Add scope or price when any of these materially increase work:

- unsupported/custom source connector;
- inherited permission disorder;
- high content churn or no source owners;
- more than one legal entity or tenant;
- personal, confidential, regulated, or safety-related information;
- many permission groups or cross-company collaborators;
- after-hours or short response commitments;
- workflow action, rollback, or financial consequence;
- nonstandard retention, residency, audit, or contract obligations;
- high user turnover or training cadence.

## Unit economics and capacity

Track every account from the first review:

| Measure | Formula | Initial threshold |
|---|---|---:|
| Assessment gross margin | (fee − direct delivery cost) / fee | 60% |
| Launch gross margin | (fee − direct delivery cost) / fee | 40% first; 50% after second |
| Recurring gross margin | (MRR − direct monthly cost) / MRR | 50% by day 90; 60% steady state |
| Effective recurring rate | MRR / delivery hours | At least $250/hour at reference scope |
| Support burden | support hours / account / month | Core below 3 hours |
| Improvement utilization | used allowance / included allowance | 60–90% without chronic overflow |
| Licence exposure | unrecovered third-party cost / MRR | Below 10% |

At the sponsor-approved half-time founder capacity, plan around 20 hours per week. Use 60% of that as the maximum sustained billable/delivery load. The balance is required for sales, productization, administration, research, and incidents. During early pilots, temporarily allocating up to 9 delivery hours per week is safer than selling to theoretical full utilization. Do not schedule more than two simultaneous launches without funded delivery help.

## Discount and deal rules

- Change scope before reducing price.
- Label founding-customer prices, expiry, and reference obligations explicitly.
- Never discount recurring fees permanently in exchange for a vague future case study.
- No “unlimited AI,” unlimited support, or unlimited development language.
- Do not hide licence or usage exposure inside a fixed fee without a defined allowance and overage rule.
- Do not guarantee savings until the baseline, measurement method, and customer dependencies are agreed.
- Require an executive sponsor, knowledge owner, access owner, and timely approval obligations in the order form.
- Preserve portability and offboarding. Customer dependence should come from value, not technical captivity.

## Alternative commercial models preserved for testing

| Model | Benefit | Constraint | When to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per-user subscription | Familiar and scales with adoption | Poor match to source and support complexity | When delivery is highly standardized |
