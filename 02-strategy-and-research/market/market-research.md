# Market research and strategic implications

**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**Status:** Secondary research updated for strategy v0.2; primary customer and MSP research not started

## Research question

Is there a defensible, sellable managed service around company knowledge when major AI and productivity platforms increasingly include enterprise search, connectors, citations, permissions, agents, and administration?

## Evidence

### Adoption is growing, but relevance and trust are unresolved

Statistics Canada reported that 19.2% of Canadian employer businesses used AI to produce goods or deliver services in the 12 months preceding its Q2 2026 survey, up from 12.2% in Q2 2025 and 6.1% in Q2 2024. Among users, common uses included data analytics, text analytics, and virtual agents/chatbots. Among barriers, 40.0% said AI was not relevant, 13.4% cited cybersecurity or privacy, and 10.6% cited cost. [Statistics Canada, Q2 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260527/dq260527a-eng.htm)

**Inference:** market education cannot be a generic case for AI. Dirty Works needs to make one operational problem relevant, control trust risk, and show value in a bounded use.

### Alberta SMBs already buy outside expertise

Statistics Canada reported that 53.7% of Alberta businesses outsourced tasks, projects, or short contracts in the 12 months preceding its Q1 2025 survey. Outsourcing rose with company size: 64.8% of businesses with 20–99 employees and 66.8% with 100 or more employees outsourced some work. Among 20–99 employee businesses, 29.3% outsourced IT support. [Statistics Canada outsourcing analysis](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2025006-eng.htm)

**Inference:** the 20–150 employee hypothesis sits in a segment accustomed to outside providers, and MSPs hold a plausible trust/distribution advantage. Outsourcing propensity does not prove willingness to buy managed knowledge operations; paid reviews must establish that.

### Professional services are a substantial but risk-sensitive lane

Alberta’s small-business profile reported that small businesses accounted for 48.9% of employment in professional, scientific, and technical services in 2023. Accounting-services data show Alberta represented 12.3% of Canadian industry operating revenue in 2024, and industry commentary identifies continuing AI-led workflow change. [Alberta Small Business Profile](https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/cc1c3dcf-d39e-4afa-9eff-32bda3759b79/resource/b60aeadc-c7bd-41ef-9115-c6b3a1f8588b/download/jet-alberta-small-business-profile-2024-10.pdf) and [Statistics Canada accounting services](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/251110/dq251110b-eng.pdf)

**Inference:** accounting and HR/advisory firms are knowledge-dense and commercially plausible, but client financial information, employee/candidate data, legal interpretation, and professional judgment make a broad “connect everything” launch inappropriate. Test internal methods and administration first.

### Energy should mean the service ecosystem, not major producers

Mining, quarrying, and oil-and-gas extraction firms reported relatively high use of outsourced accounting, law, and professional services (40.0%) in Statistics Canada’s Q1 2025 survey. Alberta’s industry profile also shows the extraction sector itself has a relatively small share of small-business employment compared with professional services. [Statistics Canada outsourcing analysis](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2025006-eng.htm) and [Alberta mining and oil-and-gas profile](https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/f4f39b9e-48cb-4f6a-b491-25ee6f9c281e/resource/ebdc160e-9a8a-459b-94dc-b3b2fedf882e/download/jet-alberta-mining-and-oil-and-gas-extraction-industry-profile-2023.pdf)

**Inference:** Dirtyworks.ai should test Calgary-area service, engineering, environmental, technology, maintenance, and project vendors around the energy sector. Major producers are likely to bring enterprise procurement and higher consequence. Initial use should remain in commercial/project administration rather than safety, engineering, operational control, or regulatory decisions.

### The product layer is rapidly commoditizing

Microsoft 365 Copilot supports work-data grounding, connectors, agents, and adoption analytics. SharePoint agents respect the user’s existing permissions. Its current business pricing is low relative to a managed-service fee. [Microsoft 365 Copilot pricing](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/pricing) and [SharePoint agents](https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/SharePoint/copilot-in-sharepoint/get-started-with-agents-in-sharepoint)

Google Workspace and Gemini can use Drive, Gmail, Chat, and Calendar sources, preserve file access controls, and provide citations. Google warns that sourced answers can still be wrong or even cite an invented source, reinforcing the need for verification and evaluation. [Google source behavior](https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/16813283?hl=en), [Google access controls](https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/17010577?hl=en), and [Gemini in Drive](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/16963068)

**Inference:** building another general chat/search interface is not an attractive first investment. Platform selection, configuration, content operations, evaluation, adoption, and cross-system workflow management remain service opportunities. The strategy must remain vendor-neutral because the best initial platform depends on the customer’s existing suite and control requirements.

### Privacy and accountability are operating requirements

Canadian privacy regulators’ joint guidance calls for necessity and proportionality, data minimization, transparency, defined accountability, impact assessments, lifecycle accuracy, traceability, and effective challenge or human-review mechanisms for significant decisions. [Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/ai-technology-and-innovation/artificial-intelligence/gd_principles_ai/)

The Alberta OIPC stated in 2025 that private-sector businesses considering AI processing of personal information should verify PIPA compliance and follow the joint commissioner guidance. It also identified monitoring, transparency, and impact assessment as useful measures while comprehensive Alberta AI legislation is absent. [Alberta OIPC responsible AI comments](https://oipc.ab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/AI-Comments-from-the-OIPC-Regarding-Responsible-AI-Governance-in-Alberta-July-15-2025.pdf)

NIST’s voluntary AI Risk Management Framework and Generative AI Profile provide a credible structure for governing, mapping, measuring, and managing AI risks. The framework is being revised, so Dirty Works should use it as an adaptable baseline rather than claim formal compliance with a static checklist. [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) and [NIST Generative AI Profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence)

**Inference:** governance is part of delivery, not a policy appendix. Initial offers should avoid significant automated decisions and sensitive/regulated domains until Dirty Works has legal review, insurance, security evidence, and mature controls.

## Competitive landscape

| Category | What customers can buy | Strategic implication |
|---|---|---|
| Productivity suites | Microsoft 365 Copilot, Google Workspace with Gemini | Use the existing tenant when it meets the job; sell operation, not displacement |
| General AI workspaces | ChatGPT Business/Enterprise and similar tools | Fast launch path for some clients; control gaps must be assessed per use case |
| Enterprise search platforms | Glean and other cross-system search products | Strong alternative for larger accounts; likely excessive for the first SMB cohort |
| Knowledge systems | Notion, Guru, Confluence, SharePoint and vertical systems with AI features | Source ownership and information architecture still determine answer quality |
| Traditional MSPs | Licences, security, identity, support, Copilot enablement | Potential channel and competitor; define the seam between IT operations and knowledge operations |
| AI consultancies | Strategy, custom RAG, agents, and integrations | Differentiate through bounded offers and ongoing accountable operations |
| Internal champion | DIY setup using native tools | Dirty Works must outperform the customer’s opportunity cost, not merely software pricing |
| Inaction | Employees continue searching and asking experts | Primary competitor; quantify recent events and avoid abstract category selling |

Current AI-channel vendors visibly market referral, co-branded, and full white-label programs, including examples with a 25% partner margin. Those pages are vendor claims, not neutral benchmarks, but they show that MSPs are being offered turnkey AI practices and will compare Dirtyworks.ai against them. [Matchbook AI MSP program](https://www.matchbookai.com/msp-partner/) and [Elevated AI partner models](https://elevatedai.io/partners/msp-ai-services)

**Inference:** partner economics must reflect who performs sales, account management, tier-1 support, delivery, and risk. A wholesale discount cannot be copied from a software reseller model when Dirtyworks.ai still supplies material labour.

## Pricing research interpretation

Software price is a poor anchor for the managed service. Current per-user AI licences can be tens of dollars per month while Dirty Works’ proposed recurring fees are thousands. The gap must be justified by accountable work: source governance, security review, evaluations, training, support, reporting, vendor change management, and improvement.

Three pricing mechanisms are plausible:

1. **Per user:** simple, but weakly connected to delivery complexity and can punish adoption.
2. **Cost plus / hours:** protects early margin, but sells labour and creates billing friction.
3. **Managed scope:** base fee determined by domains, source systems, users, risk, support level, workflows, and change allowance.

The strategy selects managed-scope pricing with internal effort guardrails. Outcome-based pricing is deferred until Dirty Works can measure an outcome it materially controls.

## Market hypotheses requiring primary research

| Hypothesis | Test | Pass signal |
|---|---|---|
| One professional-services or energy-services subsegment recognizes repeated-answer pain | Two sequential 10-interview cohorts | At least 5 independently describe a similar costly domain |
| A paid assessment is acceptable | Offer it in qualified interviews | 3 paid reviews without bundling it free |
| $2,500–$4,500 monthly is supportable | Price interviews and real proposals | At least 2 launches convert without custom discounting |
| Buyers prefer customer-owned technology | Present ownership alternatives | Clear preference or objections captured across 10 interviews |
| Knowledge service is valuable without automation | 90-day launch measurement | Repeated use plus one improved operating measure |
| MSPs will refer rather than block | 5 partner interviews after direct proof | 2 request a defined co-managed offer |
| The live MSP can produce a real paid pilot rather than general channel interest | Partner discovery and named-account proposal | One customer enters a paid review under explicit partner terms |

## Research gaps

- Alberta target-account count by employee, revenue, and chosen professional/energy-services subsegment.
- Vertical-specific software and source-system prevalence.
- Quantified cost of repeated questions, onboarding delays, and key-person dependency in the target cohort.
- Local competitor offers, prices, proof, and security posture.
- Buyer procurement, insurance, data-location, and contract expectations.
- Sensitivity of willingness-to-pay to company size, number of sources, and risk class.
- Live MSP portfolio fit, required white-label structure, tier-1 capability, desired margin, and named pilot account.

These gaps do not block the first interview campaign. They are tracked in the implementation backlog and should be resolved before committing meaningful product-development or paid-acquisition capital.
