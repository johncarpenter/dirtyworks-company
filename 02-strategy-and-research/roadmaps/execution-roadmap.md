# Phased execution roadmap

**Version:** 0.2  
**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**Status:** Phase 0 in progress

## Sequencing rule

Strategy comes first, then evidence, then repeatable delivery, then tooling, then optional product. A phase advances on evidence, not elapsed time. Work from later phases may be prototyped when it removes a current blocker, but it must not displace the current gate.

## Phase 0 — Foundation

**Purpose:** make the company thesis executable and auditable.  
**Target duration:** 1–2 weeks.  
**Current state:** core strategy, validation toolkit, brand direction, capacity/capital plan, and MSP channel design created; first direct subsegment and live-partner structure remain.

### Deliverables

- master strategy, research, offer/pricing, GTM, and delivery model;
- company-building log and implementation backlog;
- interview guide, evidence-capture template, and ICP scorecard;
- one-page private conversation brief;
- approved Dirtyworks.ai brand and testable message architecture;
- initial target-account research method;
- security/privacy/legal preflight list;
- half-time founder capacity and evidence-gated $5,000–$50,000 experiment capital plan;
- MSP channel strategy and partner discovery plan.

### Gate

- sponsor confirms the first concentrated direct subsegment;
- founder can explain the offer without relying on technical terms;
- interview and evidence capture are ready;
- no material legal/security claim is presented publicly without review.

### Stop condition

Do not build the public website or customer application merely to avoid customer conversations.

## Phase 1 — Problem and willingness-to-pay validation

**Purpose:** prove that a specific buyer recognizes and funds the problem.  
**Target duration:** 8–10 weeks at half-time founder capacity.

### Work

- select the first 10-interview cohort from accounting, HR/advisory, or energy-service/vendor SMBs;
- research 50 named accounts and up to 30 warm paths in that subsegment;
- conduct 10 recent-event interviews, synthesize the evidence, then choose and conduct a second 10-interview cohort;
- run MSP partner discovery, qualify the live opportunity, and identify one named pilot customer;
- test three message frames: answer access/continuity, onboarding/standardization, and governed AI;
- propose the paid Knowledge Reliability Review to qualified accounts;
- complete 3–5 paid reviews;
- record loss, urgency, price, source, and risk evidence.

### Gate

- at least 5 interviewees independently describe a materially similar repeated problem;
- at least 3 customers pay for the review;
- at least 2 reviews reveal a feasible bounded launch with useful existing sources;
- at least one operating baseline can be measured.
- the live MSP opportunity either produces a commercially plausible named-customer path or is explicitly deprioritized.

### Decision branches

- **Problem recognized, no payment:** revise urgency, buyer, trust, or paid-review value.
- **Payment, sources poor:** create and price a knowledge-capture service; do not promise fast RAG.
- **Strong horizontal pattern:** preserve evidence, but complete the first vertical cohort before broadening.
- **No repeatable pattern:** test one adjacent service vertical or a workflow-first offer.
- **Partner interest without a customer:** do not fund broad white-label enablement; retain a referral relationship.

## Phase 2 — Pilot delivery and proof

**Purpose:** prove reliable use and delivery economics.  
**Target duration:** 6–10 weeks per initial cohort.

### Work

- launch two bounded production accounts, potentially one direct and one MSP-sourced;
- prefer customer-owned native platforms unless a requirement disproves fit;
- execute permission, source, answer, citation, abstention, and adversarial tests;
- run a limited cohort, training, and 30-day stabilization;
- measure time-to-answer, expert interruptions, or another agreed outcome;
- track every delivery and support hour;
- produce an approved case study from at least one account;
- convert successful launches to six-month managed service.

### Gate

- no unresolved critical permission/security failures;
- repeated voluntary use by the intended cohort;
- one agreed operating measure improves materially or the buyer validates another concrete value;
- at least 2 accounts accept recurring service;
- day-90 recurring gross margin can plausibly exceed 50%;
- offboarding and portability are demonstrated.

### Stop condition

Do not generalize from a polished demo. If users do not return or the operating result does not improve, diagnose source, job, workflow, trust, and adoption before adding features.

## Phase 3 — Repeatability and business-in-a-box v1

**Purpose:** turn founder craft into a documented, investable operating system.  
**Target duration:** months 3–9.

### Work

- reach five managed customers in the same or closely related vertical;
- standardize review, proposal, contract schedule, launch, evaluation, support, reporting, and offboarding kits;
- build internal tools only for repeated high-cost tasks;
- publish the website after message evidence and proof exist;
- establish CRM, pipeline, service desk, knowledge register, time/cost tracking, invoicing, and KPI reporting;
- complete legal contract review, insurance, privacy/security baseline, and vendor due diligence;
- document founder-independent role instructions;
- test one carefully bounded managed workflow expansion.
- validate partner tier-1/tier-2 operations and actual wholesale margin if the MSP pilot proceeds.

### Gate

- 5 managed customers;
- 2 referenceable case studies;
- 60% target recurring gross margin is achieved or a credible remediation plan exists;
- onboarding checklist is complete without relying on founder memory;
- recurring operations and financial reporting run monthly;
- no single customer is more than 40% of recurring revenue.

## Phase 4 — Scale the service

**Purpose:** add capacity and distribution without losing control.  
**Target duration:** months 9–18.

### Work

- hire/contract the first knowledge operations delivery role based on bottleneck evidence;
- establish quality review and account-capacity limits;
- test MSP referral or co-managed partnerships with explicit responsibility maps;
- create a partner enablement and wholesale model only after direct margins are known;
- add vertical-specific evaluation libraries and source playbooks;
- reach 10 core-equivalent managed accounts or $45,000 MRR;
- reduce founder delivery dependence while preserving founder-led strategic sales.

### Gate

- new delivery staff can operate two accounts using the documented system;
- quality and incident measures do not materially degrade;
- customer acquisition and onboarding are repeatable;
- concentration falls below 25% for the largest account;
- the next hire is supported by contracted recurring margin.

## Phase 5 — Product decision

**Purpose:** decide whether proprietary software improves the business.  
**Earliest trigger:** evidence from at least 5 managed customers and the same recurring platform gap in at least 3.

### Productize only if

- the job is repeated and stable across customers;
- existing platforms fail a documented requirement;
- the component reduces delivery cost, improves reliability, or creates sellable differentiation;
- security, tenancy, support, migration, and lifecycle cost are funded;
- a service/process/template cannot solve the problem more cheaply;
- customers or partners demonstrate willingness to pay.

### Likely early internal tools

- evaluation-set and regression runner;
- source/owner/freshness register;
- permission-test evidence collector;
- monthly value report generator;
- customer configuration and offboarding pack generator;
- delivery time, cost, and margin dashboard.

These should begin as narrow internal tools. A multi-tenant customer application is a separate investment decision.

## Capital deployment order

1. Customer discovery and founder selling time.
2. Legal, insurance, privacy, and security foundations required for production.
3. Repeatable delivery assets and customer-owned platform configuration.
4. Evidence-backed website and sales materials.
5. Internal tooling that removes measured delivery cost or risk.
6. Hiring supported by contracted recurring revenue.
7. Proprietary platform only after the product gate.

## Investability package

The “business in a box” is complete when an investor or operator can inspect:

- strategy and market evidence;
- ICP and validated problem;
- offers, pricing, unit economics, and cohort margins;
- pipeline, sales stages, conversion, and cycle time;
- contracts, risks, controls, insurance, and vendor dependencies;
- delivery playbooks, service levels, QA, incidents, and capacity;
- customer references, retention, expansion, and outcome evidence;
- team roles, hiring triggers, and founder dependencies;
- tools/applications with ownership and operating costs;
- financial model, cash plan, and capital use.

The box is an operating system with evidence—not merely a brand, website, and forecast.
