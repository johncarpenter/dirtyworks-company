# MSP channel strategy

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**Status:** Ready for discovery with the live MSP opportunity; commercial terms are hypotheses

## Strategic role

Traditional MSPs already hold trust, tenant access, security context, contracts, and recurring relationships with the SMBs Dirtyworks.ai wants to serve. Many can configure licences and infrastructure but may not want to build a knowledge-quality, adoption, evaluation, governance, and workflow-improvement practice.

That creates a credible route to market. It also creates channel risk: Dirtyworks.ai can become an invisible labour supplier with weak margins, unclear liability, no customer learning, and dependence on one partner. The partner model must therefore specify who sells, contracts, discovers, approves, implements, supports, reports, bears risk, and owns the renewal.

## Recommended validation posture

Test the live MSP opportunity now through a **partner discovery and one-customer design pilot**. Prefer a transparent co-managed or “powered by Dirtyworks.ai” structure for the first account because direct access to users, source owners, and outcome evidence is necessary to improve the offer.

Full white-label remains a real option when the partner has a firm requirement, but it should carry:

- a wholesale price that preserves delivery margin;
- minimum partner volume or a paid enablement fee;
- explicit permission for Dirtyworks.ai to work directly with designated customer contacts while remaining under the partner brand;
- customer-approved subcontractor and data-processing disclosure where required;
- a no-solicitation clause rather than a restriction that prevents necessary delivery contact;
- clear tier-1 versus tier-2/3 support and incident obligations;
- rights to use anonymized operational patterns and separately approved case-study claims;
- no geographic or vertical exclusivity without committed minimum revenue.

## Three partner models

| Model | Customer contract | Customer-facing brand | Sales/account owner | Dirtyworks.ai role | Partner economics | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Referral | Dirtyworks.ai | Dirtyworks.ai | Dirtyworks.ai after introduction | Full delivery and service | 10% of collected first-year service revenue hypothesis | Partner wants no delivery or liability |
| Co-managed / co-branded | Either party; one prime named | Both, with explicit seam | Shared | Knowledge/AI operations specialist | 15–25% of relevant revenue or defined account fee | First pilots and complex accounts |
| Full white-label wholesale | MSP | MSP only to end customer | MSP | Subcontracted delivery and tier-2/3 operations | MSP retains 25% at reference volume hypothesis | Mature partner with pipeline and tier-1 capability |

These economics are starting points, not industry standards or promised terms. Calculate each deal from actual delivery cost and responsibility.

## Partner qualification

Score 0–2 on each dimension:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relevant SMB base | Few/no target accounts | Some adjacent accounts | Concentrated target portfolio |
| Executive sponsorship | Opportunistic salesperson | Interested practice lead | Owner/executive commits |
| Account trust | Transactional/licence-only | Mixed | Strategic recurring relationships |
| Security/identity maturity | Weak/unclear | Basic | Documented controls and escalation |
| Sales capability | Wants leads from Dirtyworks.ai | Will co-sell | Can qualify and sell the offer |
| Tier-1 support | None | Limited | Owns intake, access basics, and status |
| Customer access | Blocks all access | Mediated | Enables delivery contact with clear rules |
| Economics | Demands margin without work | Negotiable | Margin aligned to responsibilities/volume |
| Learning/reference | No evidence access | Anonymized data | Allows outcome measurement and approved proof |
| Strategic conflict | Plans competing practice immediately | Unclear | Explicit complementary seam |

Pilot threshold: 14/20 with no zero in executive sponsorship, security/identity maturity, customer access, or economics.

## Commercial model

### Referral hypothesis

- 10% of collected assessment, launch, and recurring service revenue for the first 12 months.
- Paid after Dirtyworks.ai receives customer payment.
- No commission on pass-through licences, taxes, travel, legal specialists, or reimbursable expenses.
- Dirtyworks.ai controls scope, price, contracting, and service.

### Co-managed hypothesis

- Price each party’s scope separately or allocate 15–25% to the MSP based on sales, account management, tenant work, tier-1 support, and collections.
- The customer sees the responsibility map and escalation seam.
- Avoid duplicate “unlimited support” promises across two contracts.

### White-label wholesale hypothesis

Reference buy rates at entry volume:

| Direct reference price | Initial partner buy-rate hypothesis | Partner headroom at same resale price |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $2,500 review | $1,875 | $625 / 25% |
| $9,500 launch | $7,125 | $2,375 / 25% |
| $2,500 monthly Core | $1,875 | $625 / 25% |
| $4,500 monthly Operate | $3,375 | $1,125 / 25% |

These rates work only if the MSP owns lead generation, sales, account management, collections, and tier-1 support, and if Dirtyworks.ai’s reduced delivery cost preserves its internal margin floor. Otherwise the wholesale discount must shrink.

Volume tiers should follow observed efficiencies, not promises. A possible later structure is 25% at 1–4 active accounts and 30% at 5+ standardized accounts, subject to minimum term and on-time payment. No tier should push Dirtyworks.ai recurring gross margin below 50%, with 60% still the operating target.

## White-label enablement fee

A partner asking for branded collateral, sales training, custom portal/email behavior, contract alignment, sandbox/demo configuration, and staff certification is requesting a launch project. Test a **$5,000–$10,000 partner enablement fee**, creditable in part against the first two launches, rather than funding bespoke channel setup on speculation.

Waive or reduce it only when the partner brings a signed pilot customer or makes a minimum revenue commitment.

## Pilot design

### Stage 1 — Partner discovery

Conduct a 60-minute working session covering:

- customer profile and current AI requests;
- current Microsoft/Google/cloud/security practice;
- why the partner wants white-label versus referral or co-managed delivery;
- available pilot account and recent customer problem;
- sales, contracting, billing, support, and customer-access expectations;
- security, insurance, privacy, data, and subcontractor requirements;
- desired margin and work the partner will perform;
- exclusivity, references, and roadmap expectations.

### Stage 2 — Internal fit and economics

- score partner qualification;
- map roles and escalation;
- model direct delivery cost at proposed wholesale rate;
- select a low-risk customer answer domain;
- identify contract/DPA/insurance review;
- decide referral, co-managed, or white-label structure.

### Stage 3 — One-customer paid pilot

- the Knowledge Reliability Review remains paid;
- partner and customer attend the scope/ownership session;
- Dirtyworks.ai receives the access needed to conduct valid discovery and evaluation;
- no exclusivity;
- pilot has launch, recurring-price, evidence, offboarding, and stop terms;
- review after 90 days before expanding to the partner’s base.

## Partner metrics

- qualified partner opportunities;
- time from partner discovery to named customer;
- partner-sourced paid reviews and launches;
- sell-through and sales cycle;
- partner discount and Dirtyworks.ai gross margin;
- tier-1 deflection versus escalations;
- customer quality/outcome measures;
- revenue and pipeline concentration by partner;
- renewal, expansion, and partner inactivity;
- channel conflict or customer-access incidents.

## Stop or redesign conditions

- partner will not identify a real pilot customer;
- partner requests extensive enablement without payment or commitment;
- Dirtyworks.ai cannot access users/source owners enough to deliver responsibly;
- proposed wholesale price falls below the margin floor;
- partner promises a broader or higher-risk service than Dirtyworks.ai can operate;
- subcontractor secrecy conflicts with law, contract, platform terms, or incident response;
- exclusivity is requested without minimum committed revenue;
- the partner blocks outcome evidence and leaves Dirtyworks.ai unable to improve the service;
- one partner would exceed 40% of revenue before the operating model is proven.

## Contract and legal issues for counsel

- prime contractor, subcontractor, reseller, agent, and data-processing roles;
- brand use, sales claims, proposal approval, and customer communications;
- confidentiality, non-solicitation, non-circumvention, and reasonable limits;
- privacy/data-processing instructions, subprocessors, location, retention, and breach cooperation;
- IP ownership for customer content, configurations, evaluation sets, generalized methods, and custom work;
- service levels, upstream vendor exclusions, support handoffs, and maintenance windows;
- payment timing, bad debt, taxes, refunds, price changes, and pass-through cost;
- indemnities, liability caps, insurance, prohibited/high-risk uses, and customer responsibilities;
- termination, direct-transition restrictions, portability, data deletion, and customer continuity;
- exclusivity only when narrow, time-bound, and backed by minimum performance.

This document provides commercial design, not legal advice.
