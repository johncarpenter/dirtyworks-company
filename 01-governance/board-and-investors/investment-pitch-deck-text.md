## How to use this document

Each slide contains audience-facing copy, speaker notes, and the supporting exhibit or source. The audience-facing section is the text that belongs on the slide. Speaker notes provide the argument, caveats, and handoff detail.

The deck is deliberately not written as a venture-SaaS pitch. Dirtyworks.ai is currently a capital-efficient, founder-led managed-service experiment with a possible software/productization path only after repeated delivery work reveals one.

## 02 — The problem

### Audience-facing copy

> **YOUR COMPANY KNOWS MORE THAN IT CAN FIND.**

The answer is usually somewhere.

- The current procedure lives in an old folder.
- The last project solved this, but nobody can find it.
- Two teams are using different versions.
- New staff keep asking the same experienced people.
- AI tools are already in the workflow. Nobody owns the operating result.

**Searching, asking, waiting, recreating. The work still stops.**

### Speaker notes

Dirtyworks.ai does not begin with an AI category pitch. It begins with a recent operating event: an answer that had to be chased, verified, recreated, or escalated.

The experienced employee is not the problem. They are carrying an invisible knowledge system the company has not supported. The economic case must be tied to elapsed time, interruptions, rework, onboarding, continuity, response delay, or another measurable operating consequence.

## 03 — Why now

### Audience-facing copy

> **AI ACCESS IS RISING. OPERATING CONFIDENCE IS NOT KEEPING PACE.**

**19.2%** of Canadian employer businesses used AI in the year preceding Q2 2026.  
**3.1×** the Q2 2024 adoption rate.

But businesses still report relevance, privacy, cybersecurity, and cost barriers.

At the same time, Alberta SMBs already buy outside expertise: **53.7%** of Alberta businesses outsourced work in the year preceding Q1 2025.

**The software layer is becoming easier to buy. The operating layer still needs an owner.**

### Speaker notes

Statistics Canada reported AI adoption of 19.2% in Q2 2026, compared with 12.2% in Q2 2025 and 6.1% in Q2 2024. Among businesses not planning AI use, 40.0% said it was not relevant; cybersecurity/privacy and cost were also barriers.

The opening is not “more AI.” It is making one bounded use relevant, controlled, measurable, and operable.

Outsourcing data supports the plausibility of an outside managed-service model, particularly for 20–99 employee businesses. It does not prove willingness to buy this offer; paid reviews must establish that.

## 04 — The solution

### Audience-facing copy

> **THE INTERFACE IS NOT THE OPERATION.**

Dirtyworks.ai makes approved company knowledge easier to use—and keeps the system managed.

`SOURCE` Who owns the current information?  
`ACCESS` Who should be able to retrieve it?  
`PROOF` Does the answer meet the agreed test?  
`FAILURE` What happens when it is missing or contradictory?  
`CHANGE` Who retests the system on Monday morning?

**Customer-owned technology and data by default. Human accountability where consequence remains human.**

### Speaker notes

The first implementation uses the customer’s incumbent platform when it meets the job. Dirtyworks.ai does not need to build a general search or chatbot product to create value.

The initial scope is one answer domain, one role group, and one to three approved source systems. The service includes source ownership, permission testing, evaluations based on real questions, rollout, support, incidents, reporting, and ongoing improvement.

## 05 — The commercial wedge

### Audience-facing copy

> **START SMALL ENOUGH TO PROVE. EXPAND ONLY WHEN THE WORK EARNS IT.**

**01 / REVIEW — $2,500**  
Decide whether one knowledge domain is feasible, valuable, and responsible.

**02 / LAUNCH — $9,500 reference scope**  
Put one bounded domain into production and establish reliability, use, and ownership.

**03 / OPERATE — $2,500 / $4,500 / $7,500+ monthly**  
Run sources, permissions, evaluation, support, reporting, incidents, and improvement.

**04 / EXPAND — separately scoped**  
Repair knowledge, add domains, integrate systems, assist work, or automate proven workflows.

### Speaker notes

Every step can produce value without forcing the next. Diagnosis is paid; implementation uses milestone billing; recurring service is billed in advance. Customer licences and pass-through costs stay separate.

This ladder protects cash and makes the sales funnel measurable. It also prevents a broad transformation promise from hiding a weak first use case.

## 06 — Market entry

### Audience-facing copy

> **A NARROW BEACHHEAD. A REPEATABLE OPERATING PROBLEM.**

**Direct customers**  
20–150 employee Alberta businesses in:

- accounting and HR/advisory services;
- Calgary-area energy-service, engineering, environmental, technology, maintenance, and project firms.

**Channel customers**  
Traditional MSPs serving the same companies.

**One customer can represent approximately $30K–$90K+ recurring annual service revenue, before launch or expansion work.**

**Target-account count is still being measured. No inflated TAM is presented as fact.**

### Speaker notes

The beachhead is defined by operating fit, not a top-down market percentage. A qualifying customer has repeated-answer pain, useful approved sources, a named sponsor and knowledge owner, a bounded low-risk first use, and willingness to measure an operating result.

The bottom-up market-sizing method is:

```text
qualified target accounts
× obtainable recurring annual service revenue
+ expected review, launch and expansion revenue
```

The account count by subsegment is a known research gap and should be resolved before paid acquisition or a geographic expansion claim.

## 07 — Business model

### Audience-facing copy

> **RECURRING REVENUE COMES FROM RESPONSIBILITY—not consumption markup.**

| Revenue | Economic role |
|---|---|
| Paid review | Funds diagnosis and qualifies implementation |
| Launch | Funds customer-specific configuration and release |
| Managed service | Pays for the ongoing operating responsibility |
| Expansion | Monetizes evidence-qualified improvement work |
| Partner enablement | Prevents speculative channel setup from becoming free labour |

**Licences remain customer-paid. Scope—not tokens—sets the price.**

### Speaker notes

The model avoids hiding third-party licence exposure inside a fixed fee. Price is governed by domains, sources, permissions, users, workflows, support, risk, and change.

Managed-service durability depends on recurring labour becoming standardized enough to achieve at least 60% normalized gross margin. Automation expansion is not required to subsidize the core offer.

## 08 — Go to market

### Audience-facing copy

> **SELL THE LAST COSTLY ANSWER—not an AI FUTURE.**

**Direct motion**  
Warm introduction → recent-event interview → qualification → paid review → launch → managed operation.

**MSP motion**  
Partner discovery → responsibility and economics map → named paid pilot → 90-day review → repeatable partner offer.

**First validation gate**

- 20 buyer interviews;
- 3 paid reviews without free bundling;
- 1 MSP-sourced paid review;
- at least 2 feasible launches.

### Speaker notes

Founder-led selling is a deliberate learning system at this stage. The founder must hear how buyers describe the problem, why they do or do not pay, and which responsibility they expect an outside operator to own.

Referral, co-managed, and full white-label models remain real options. The first partner pilot should preserve direct access to users, source owners, and outcome evidence. Full white-label requires a margin-preserving buy rate, clear support tiers, and either a paid enablement fee or committed revenue.

## 09 — The operating advantage

### Audience-facing copy

> **THE MOAT IS A BETTER WAY TO OPERATE THE INVISIBLE WORK.**

Dirtyworks.ai compounds a portable operating system:

- source inventory, ownership, classification, and freshness;
- permission design and regression tests;
- real-question evaluation sets and release gates;
- abstention, contradiction, and escalation paths;
- adoption, support, incident, change, and offboarding routines;
- vertical question patterns and workflow qualification methods.

**Customer content stays customer-confidential. Generalized operating knowledge compounds.**

### Speaker notes

Native platforms will continue to improve. That commoditizes the interface and strengthens the case for a vendor-neutral operator.

The defensible assets are repeatable controls, delivery evidence, vertical understanding, customer trust, and operational records that make each subsequent launch more reliable and less labour-intensive. Proprietary software becomes justified only when the same stable, margin-limiting job appears across customers.

## 10 — Unit economics

### Audience-facing copy

> **THE ECONOMICS ARE PLAUSIBLE. THEY ARE NOT YET CLEAN.**

Base-case direct normalized gross margin:

| Offer | Margin | Current target |
|---|---:|---:|
| Review | 57.5% | 60% |
| Launch | 44.9% | 50% after the second |
| Core | 56.5% | 60% |
| Operate | 60.8% | 60% |
| Scale | 62.2% | 60% |

**Before scaling:** reduce delivery hours, change scope, raise price, or require partner work that actually removes cost.

### Speaker notes

These margins include founder delivery time at $125 per hour, direct account cost, and a payment/bad-debt reserve. They exclude overhead.

The model is intentionally strict. Review, Launch, and Core miss their normalized targets in the base case. This is not a fatal result; it is the current validation agenda.

At the base assumptions, a 25% channel discount without cost reduction pushes Review, Launch, Core, and Operate below their desired margin floors. The Channel Economics sheet tests whether real partner responsibilities reduce cost enough to restore margin. For example, the modeled white-label Operate package reaches 53.5% normalized margin with a 10% delivery-cost reduction; the white-label Launch remains only 34.3%.

## 11 — Financial scenarios

### Audience-facing copy

> **THE BASE CASE BUILDS A REAL SERVICE BUSINESS—not A VENTURE FICTION.**

| Month 18 | Downside | Base | Upside |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Managed accounts | 0.8 | 4.5 | 10.3 |
| MRR | $1.8K | $18.9K | $56.5K |
| Normalized gross margin | 7.8% | 58.4% | 76.4% |

Base case by month 36:

**$55.3K MRR · 13.3 managed accounts · $1.15M cumulative revenue · $33.3K monthly normalized operating profit**

### Speaker notes

All three cases use an internally coherent review-to-launch-to-managed funnel, churn, package mix, channel discount, staged delivery capacity, and cash collection.

The base case reaches about 4.5 active managed accounts and $18.9K MRR in month 18—slightly below the strategy target of $20K–$30K. That gap must be closed through stronger package mix, acquisition, price, retention, or expansion rather than a spreadsheet override.

Fractional contracts represent planning averages; real sales will be uneven. The model does not include terminal value, income tax, GST, debt, depreciation, or pass-through licence revenue.

## 12 — Capacity and the hiring mechanism

### Audience-facing copy

> **DEMAND BECOMES A DELIVERY PROBLEM BEFORE IT BECOMES A CASH PROBLEM.**

Half-time founder capacity:

**20 hours/week · 60% maximum sustained delivery · approximately 52 delivery hours/month**

Base-case capacity plan:

- core contractor begins in month 15;
- 80 contractor hours/month;
- flex subcontracting covers overflow at a visible margin cost;
- peak flex need reaches approximately **82 hours/month** by month 36.

**The hiring gate is contracted workload plus normalized margin—not optimism.**

### Speaker notes

The model does not allow demand to become magically deliverable. Founder time fills first, then planned contractor capacity, then flex subcontracting. Flex protects customer commitments but compresses margin, making the next capacity decision visible.

The upside case requires more than 200 flex hours in its peak month even after earlier contractor capacity. That is not a forecast of heroic founder execution; it is an instruction to add a delivery lead or deliberately cap sales.

## 13 — Evidence-gated capital

### Audience-facing copy

> **CAPITAL IS RELEASED WHEN EVIDENCE REMOVES A RISK.**

**$5K — validate and prepare**  
Interviews, partner discovery, basic legal/accounting orientation, prototype assets.

**+$10K–$15K — production readiness**  
Release after paid-review and feasible-launch evidence.

**+$20K–$30K — repeatable delivery**  
Release after two accepted launches, recurring contracts, measured value, and a credible margin path.

**Base model:** $42.5K planned sponsor capital plus approximately **$2.2K** of cash-timing headroom.

### Speaker notes

This is not a request to pre-fund $50,000. It is a capital allocation mechanism.

The base case still reaches a minimum cash balance of negative $2.2K after planned tranches. Real options are to add a small reserve, improve up-front collections, defer one-time spend, slow compensation, or change the acquisition/capacity plan. The model leaves that choice open.

## 14 — What can break the thesis

### Audience-facing copy

> **THE BUSINESS HAS AN EXPLICIT STOP BUTTON.**

Redesign or stop when:

- buyers describe the problem but will not pay for diagnosis;
- reviews do not convert into feasible launches;
- launches do not produce repeated use or an improved operating measure;
- recurring labour remains proportional to exceptions and cannot reach 50% gross margin;
- native platforms solve the job well enough without managed operations;
- privacy, security, insurance, or contract cost exceeds the target price;
- a partner controls the customer but does not remove enough work to justify its discount.

### Speaker notes

The largest current risks are not technical. They are problem recognition, willingness to pay, source readiness, founder capacity, channel economics, and the possibility that native platforms erase the service wedge.

Every risk has a near-term test. The company should stop an offer or segment before building custom software to rescue it.

## 15 — Close

### Audience-facing copy

> **A CAPITAL-EFFICIENT TEST OF A DURABLE OPERATING NEED.**

Dirtyworks.ai becomes a strong business if it proves four things:

1. a narrow customer segment repeatedly pays to fix the knowledge problem;
2. a bounded launch becomes a retained managed operation;
3. normalized recurring gross margin reaches 60%;
4. MSP distribution adds qualified demand while preserving responsibility, learning, and margin.

**The next milestone is not a funding round. It is three paid reviews and one named MSP pilot.**

### Speaker notes

The founder brings CTO and company-operating experience, which is relevant to trust, executive selling, technical judgment, and operating-model design. Add specific companies, scope, outcomes, and sector relationships only when the founder approves them for public use.

This is a deliberately reversible experiment. If the four proofs emerge, capital can accelerate capacity, controls, and distribution. If they do not, the company has preserved cash and learned where the model breaks.
